# Worksheet Wonder: Illustration Guide

## 1. Core Aesthetic Principles
Illustrations are not merely decorative; they are functional educational tools. The style must be instantly recognizable, universally appealing, and perfectly suited for both digital viewing and home-printer output.

*   **Format:** 100% Vector (SVG). No raster images. SVGs guarantee infinite scaling without pixelation and keep file sizes incredibly small for HTML-based print generation.
*   **Line Art:** Thick, uniform stroke widths (typically `stroke-width="2.5"` or `3`). 
    *   *Why:* Thick outlines provide a "coloring book" feel, clearly defining shapes for young eyes and ensuring lines print solidly even on low-quality inkjet printers.
*   **Shapes:** Geometric and organic blending. Avoid excessive hyper-realism. Use circles, smooth curves, and rounded polygons. 
*   **Details:** Minimalist. Remove any details that do not contribute to identifying the object. A bear needs ears, eyes, a snout, and a body shape. It does not need individual fur textures.
*   **Expression:** Where applicable (animals, mascots), faces should be friendly, expressive, and engaging.

## 2. Color vs. Black & White
Worksheet Wonder templates must look premium in both color and black-and-white.

*   **Colored SVGs:** Use solid, flat colors from the official Colour System. Avoid complex gradients or drop shadows within the SVG itself, as these can print unpredictably. Use cell-shading or distinct color blocks if depth is needed.
*   **Coloring Activity SVGs:** Every template must include a section where the child can color. 
    *   *Rules:* The SVG must use a dark slate (`#2C3E50`) thick stroke with a pure white (`#FFFFFF`) or transparent fill. Lines must fully enclose shapes so children understand where to color.

## 3. The Mascot System
To create a cohesive brand experience, the workbooks utilize a consistent mascot (e.g., an Ant or a Ladybug) that guides the child.
*   *Role:* The mascot appears in the instruction boxes or tracing checks, waving, pointing, or holding a pencil.
*   *Consistency:* The mascot's styling must match the vocabulary illustrations perfectly.

## 4. Avoidance Checklist
*   **NO** Clip-art from disparate sources (creates a cheap, Frankenstein look).
*   **NO** Emojis.
*   **NO** Thin, wispy lines that will disappear when printed.
*   **NO** overly complex backgrounds behind vocabulary words; the object must be isolated on white or a very pale shape.
