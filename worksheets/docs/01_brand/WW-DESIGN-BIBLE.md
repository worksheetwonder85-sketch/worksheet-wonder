# CHAPTER 01 — BRAND PHILOSOPHY

## 1.1 Who We Are

**Worksheet Wonder** is a premium educational publishing brand dedicated to creating the world's most beautiful, effective, and inclusive printable resources for children aged 2–12.

We exist at the precise intersection of **expert pedagogy**, **premium design**, and **genuine delight**. We are not a clip-art business. We are not a photocopied worksheet factory. We are a global educational brand that believes every child deserves materials as beautifully crafted as the best children's books on the market.

## 1.2 The Three Pillars

Every product created under the Worksheet Wonder name must honour all three pillars simultaneously:

### Pillar 1 — EDUCATE
Every page must teach something specific, measurable, and developmentally appropriate. There must be a documented learning objective. There must be a skill being built. Entertainment alone is never enough.

### Pillar 2 — DELIGHT
The moment a child picks up a Worksheet Wonder sheet, they should feel excited. The design should spark curiosity, warmth, and energy. A worksheet that looks like homework has already failed. Ours feel like an invitation.

### Pillar 3 — EMPOWER
Every activity must leave the child feeling more capable than before they started. Difficulty must be calibrated so that success is achievable but not trivial. Confidence is the product we are ultimately selling.

# CHAPTER 02 — EDUCATIONAL PHILOSOPHY

## 2.1 Our Pedagogical Foundation

Worksheet Wonder subscribes to an **evidence-based, child-centred** approach to learning material design. Our resources are not created by graphic designers who happen to know children exist. They are created by educational designers who understand how children learn, and then made beautiful.

## 2.2 Core Pedagogical Principles

### Principle 1 — One Skill Per Page
Every worksheet targets exactly one learning objective. Multi-skill pages create cognitive overload. Children (and teachers) should be able to state the learning goal in a single sentence.

### Principle 2 — Gradual Release of Responsibility
Each activity sequence follows the **I Do / We Do / You Do** framework:
- **I Do:** Modelled example provided on the page
- **We Do:** Guided practice with scaffolding (dots, guides, dashes)
- **You Do:** Independent application with reduced support

### Principle 3 — Mastery Before Progression
Difficulty increases only when the prior level can be completed with confidence. Bundles are structured to reinforce mastery, not race through content.

### Principle 4 — Meaningful Context
Skills are never practised in isolation from meaning. Tracing a horizontal line is framed as a train journey. Counting is linked to objects the child recognises and loves. Context accelerates retention.

### Principle 5 — Positive Reinforcement by Design
The physical design of every page must contain built-in positive reinforcement: reward boxes, "great job!" areas, progress indicators. Motivation is a design variable.

## 2.3 Developmental Appropriateness

All content must be vetted against established developmental milestones:

| Age Range | Key Developmental Stage | Design Implication |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------------|
| 2–3 years | Pre-operational, sensorimotor | Large targets, thick lines, minimal text |
| 3–4 years | Symbolic thinking begins | Recognisable icons, simple tracing, colours |
| 4–5 years | Pre-writing, phonemic awareness | Letter readiness, rhyme, pattern recognition |
| 5–6 years | Emergent literacy/numeracy | Letter/number formation, simple counting |
| 6–8 years | Concrete operations | Multi-step tasks, reading, basic arithmetic |
| 8–12 years | Abstract thought developing | Written explanation, inference, problem-solving |

## 2.4 What Worksheets Can and Cannot Do

### Worksheets CAN:
- Provide structured practice of a skill
- Scaffold new learning with visual support
- Build automaticity through repetition
- Support assessment and progress monitoring
- Give children a tangible record of achievement

### Worksheets CANNOT:
- Replace hands-on exploration and play
- Introduce entirely new concepts without prior teaching
- Substitute for teacher–child interaction
- Serve as the sole measure of a child's ability

All Worksheet Wonder teacher guides acknowledge this balance explicitly.

## 2.5 The Learning Loop

Every Worksheet Wonder activity is designed around a four-stage loop:

```
ENGAGE --> EXPLORE --> PRACTISE --> CELEBRATE
```

- **Engage:** The themed illustration/scene captures attention
- **Explore:** The child encounters the task with curiosity
- **Practise:** The structured activity builds the skill
- **Celebrate:** The reward section affirms success

# CHAPTER 03 — CHILD PSYCHOLOGY PRINCIPLES

## 3.1 Why Psychology Drives Design

Children do not separate how something looks from how it makes them feel. A cluttered, grey, intimidating page triggers avoidance. A warm, spacious, inviting page triggers engagement. Design IS psychology in educational materials.

## 3.2 Key Psychological Principles Applied

### 3.2.1 Growth Mindset by Design
All language used on worksheets communicates that ability grows with effort:
- YES: "Keep trying — you're getting better!"
- YES: "Every line you trace makes you stronger!"
- NO: "Easy!" (implies failure if it wasn't easy)
- NO: "Simple!" (can create shame if the child struggles)

### 3.2.2 Autonomy and Agency
Children engage more deeply when they have choice:
- Where applicable, offer choice elements ("Colour your favourite!")
- Allow personal expression within the structure
- Never over-prescribe beyond what the learning objective requires

### 3.2.3 The Goldilocks Challenge Level
The task must be within the child's Zone of Proximal Development (Vygotsky) — neither too easy (boring) nor too hard (frustrating). Our difficulty ratings are calibrated to keep children in the flow state.

### 3.2.4 Social-Emotional Safety
The page must never create anxiety:
- No red "wrong answer" markers in our style
- All correction prompts are gentle and constructive
- Errors are framed as "try again" opportunities

### 3.2.5 Attachment to Characters
Children who bond with recurring characters engage more deeply and maintain effort longer. This is the basis of our Mascot System (Chapter 10).

### 3.2.6 The Power of Completion
The human brain releases dopamine upon task completion. Every Worksheet Wonder page is designed so that completion is:
- Clearly defined (there is an obvious ending)
- Visually satisfying (the completed page looks "full")
- Celebrated (the reward section marks the moment)

## 3.3 Anxiety Triggers to Avoid

| Trigger | Avoided By |
|---------|-----------|
| Overwhelming amounts of text | Maximum 3 lines of instruction |
| Too many items on a page | Maximum 8–10 activity items per page |
| Red ink / fail indicators | Only warm, neutral correction cues |
| Unclear tasks | Every activity has a worked example or visual model |
| Tight margins | Generous whitespace on all sides |
| Tiny writing spaces | Writing lines/boxes calibrated to age-appropriate hand size |

## 3.4 Colour Psychology (Black and White Adaptation)

Since our primary format is B&W print, we use tonal contrast and pattern rather than colour to convey psychological warmth:
- **Rounded shapes:** psychologically safe and approachable
- **High contrast start/end markers:** reduces cognitive load
- **Whimsical illustrations:** activates imaginative engagement
- **White space:** reduces overwhelm and signals "you can do this"

# CHAPTER 04 — LEARNING METHODOLOGY

## 4.1 Methodology Framework

Worksheet Wonder resources draw from multiple validated educational methodologies, synthesised into our own unified approach.

## 4.2 Methodologies Integrated

### 4.2.1 Direct Instruction (DI)
Clear, explicit modelling before independent practice. Every page shows an example before asking the child to attempt the task.

### 4.2.2 Scaffolded Learning
Support structures are built into the page and progressively removed:
- Level 1: Fully dotted/dashed guide with arrows
- Level 2: Dotted guide only
- Level 3: Start and end points only
- Level 4: Free attempt with minimal support

### 4.2.3 Spaced Practice
Within a bundle, skills are introduced, then revisited across multiple sheets at increasing intervals. This exploits the spacing effect to improve long-term retention.

### 4.2.4 Interleaving
Advanced bundles mix skill types within a session to strengthen flexible application of knowledge.

### 4.2.5 Retrieval Practice
Activities that require recall (rather than recognition) are marked with a specific icon in the activity header.

### 4.2.6 Multisensory Learning
Where possible, worksheets are paired with physical activities:
- Tracing in sand, in the air, or on a partner's back
- Speaking the name of each shape while tracing
- Physical movement linked to the worksheet theme

Teacher Guides always include multisensory extension suggestions.

## 4.3 Activity Type Taxonomy

Every activity in the Worksheet Wonder library is assigned one of the following types:

| Code | Type | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| TR | Trace | Follow a pre-drawn path |
| WR | Write | Produce text independently |
| CO | Colour | Apply colour within defined regions |
| MA | Match | Connect related items |
| CU | Cut | Scissor skills activity |
| SO | Sort | Categorise items by attribute |
| CT | Count | Enumerate objects or symbols |
| DR | Draw | Produce an original illustration |
| CI | Circle | Identify and mark correct answers |
| CN | Connect | Draw lines between items |
| PA | Pattern | Extend or identify a repeating sequence |
| CL | Classify | Group items by shared attribute |
| SE | Sequence | Order items chronologically or logically |
| PZ | Puzzle | Solve a structured problem |
| CM | Comprehension | Demonstrate understanding of text |

- Never sacrifice whitespace to fit one more item

# CHAPTER 06 — TYPOGRAPHY STANDARDS

## 6.1 Approved Font Families

Worksheet Wonder uses a carefully curated two-font system:

### 6.1.1 Display Font — Fredoka One
- **Use for:** Worksheet titles, chapter headings, brand name, badge text, reward section headers, certificate titles
- **Why:** Rounded letterforms feel warm and child-friendly; excellent readability at display sizes; distinctive without being distracting
- **Fallback stack:** 'Fredoka One', 'Arial Rounded MT Bold', cursive

### 6.1.2 Body Font — Nunito
- **Use for:** Instructions, objective text, labels, body copy, meta information, footer, pill badges
- **Why:** Rounded terminals create warmth; excellent readability at small sizes; OpenType features support multiple weights
- **Fallback stack:** 'Nunito', 'Arial Rounded MT Bold', Arial, sans-serif

## 6.2 Type Scale

| Role | Font | Weight | Size (pt) |
|------|------|--------|-----------|
| Worksheet title | Fredoka One | 400 | 24–30pt |
| Section heading | Fredoka One | 400 | 14–18pt |
| Subheading | Nunito | 800 | 10–12pt |
| Body / instructions | Nunito | 700 | 8–9pt |
| Label / caption | Nunito | 700 | 7–8pt |
| Micro text / footer | Nunito | 700 | 7–7.5pt |
| Row number | Nunito | 900 | 8pt |
| Pill badge | Nunito | 800 | 7.5pt |
| Section label (caps) | Nunito | 900 | 6.5–7pt |

## 6.3 Section Labels (ALLCAPS Micro Text)

Used above info strip blocks, above learning objectives, etc.

- Font: Nunito 900
- Size: 6.5–7pt
- Transform: UPPERCASE
- Letter-spacing: 1.5–2px
- Colour: #999 (B&W)

Examples:
```
LEARNING OBJECTIVE
INSTRUCTIONS
WHAT YOU NEED
```

## 6.4 Typography Rules — Absolute

1. Never use more than 2 typefaces on any page — Fredoka One + Nunito only
2. Never use bold + italic simultaneously — choose one or neither
3. Never justify text — always left-align body text, centre-align titles
4. Never use letter-spacing on body text — only on section labels
5. Minimum font size: 7pt — anything smaller is inaccessible
6. Maximum line length: 70 characters — for readability
7. Line height: 1.4–1.6 x font size for body text
8. Child-facing instructions: maximum 3 lines — brevity is kindness

## 6.5 Handwriting Fonts (Activity Content Only)

When worksheets feature letter/word tracing, the tracing font must be:
- A simple, unembellished print letterform
- No serif, no script, no decorative styling
- Baseline aligned
- Consistent stroke width throughout
- Approved styles: Sassoon Primary, Zaner-Bloser, or original SVG letterforms

## 6.6 Forbidden Typography Practices

- FORBIDDEN: Comic Sans (regardless of perceived "child-friendliness")
- FORBIDDEN: Script or cursive fonts (except where teaching cursive writing)
- FORBIDDEN: All-caps body text (more than 3 words)
- FORBIDDEN: White text on light backgrounds (contrast failure)
- FORBIDDEN: Coloured text in B&W worksheets (renders as grey, low contrast)
- FORBIDDEN: Text on top of illustrations
- FORBIDDEN: Multiple font colours on the same page (B&W format)
- FORBIDDEN: Condensed or stretched letterforms

# CHAPTER 07 — WHITE SPACE RULES

## 7.1 The Philosophy of Space

White space is not empty space. It is **active design**. In children's educational materials, white space performs four critical functions:

1. **Cognitive breathing room** — reduces overwhelm and anxiety
2. **Visual hierarchy** — guides the eye to what matters
3. **Motor workspace** — gives young hands room to write and draw
4. **Perceived quality** — spacious layouts read as premium

The single most common mistake in children's educational design is filling every pixel. Our most powerful design tool is restraint.

## 7.2 White Space Minimums

| Context | Minimum White Space |
|---------|-------------------|
| Between activity rows | 4mm |
| Around the info strip | 2mm above, 3mm below |
| Inside writing boxes | 3mm padding on all sides |
| Between title and first content | 3mm |
| Inside pill badges | 1mm vertical, 7mm horizontal |
| Inside info strip blocks | 2mm padding |
| Between border and content | 4mm clearance |

## 7.3 The 40% Rule

At minimum, **40% of any worksheet page must be white (unprinted) space**. This is a non-negotiable standard. If a page fails the 40% test, content must be removed or reorganised across multiple pages.

## 7.4 Common White Space Failures

| Failure | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Too many rows per page | Cramped, stressful, hard to write in | Remove rows, split to two pages |
| Decorative illustrations too large | Crowds activity area | Scale down or move to header only |
| Instruction text too verbose | Wastes line height | Edit ruthlessly |
| Border too thick | Reduces usable area | Cap border at 3px outer, 1.5px inner |
| Reward section too tall | Compresses activities | Keep reward box under 22mm |

## 7.5 White Space and Developmental Age

| Age Group | Recommended Content Density |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| 2–3 years | Maximum 30% coverage |
| 3–4 years | Maximum 40% coverage |
| 4–5 years | Maximum 45% coverage |
| 5–7 years | Maximum 55% coverage |
| 7–10 years | Maximum 65% coverage |
| 10–12 years | Maximum 70% coverage |

# CHAPTER 08 — BORDER RULES

## 8.1 Purpose of Borders

Borders serve a containment and aesthetic function. They frame the worksheet as a complete, professional document, signal to the child that "this is my space," reinforce brand identity, and add visual warmth without adding complexity.

They must never compete with content, distract the eye, or reduce the usable area meaningfully.

## 8.2 Standard Border System

Worksheet Wonder uses a **dual-border system**:

### Outer Border
- Weight: 3px solid
- Colour: #1a1a1a (near-black)
- Border-radius: 12–14px (rounded)
- Clearance from page edge: 4mm

### Inner Border
- Weight: 1.5px dashed
- Colour: #aaa (mid-grey)
- Border-radius: 9–10px
- Clearance from outer border: 2.5mm

### Corner Decorations
- Symbol: Four-pointed star (Unicode U+2726)
- Size: 14–16px
- Colour: #1a1a1a
- Positioned: 8–9mm from page edge

## 8.3 Border Variations by Product Type

| Product Type | Border Style |
|-------------|-------------|
| Standard worksheet | Outer solid 3px + inner dashed 1.5px |
| Cover page | Outer solid 3.5px + inner dashed 2px |
| Certificate | Outer solid 4px + inner dotted 2px (formal) |
| Poster | Outer solid 5px + decorative corner elements |
| Flashcard | Outer solid 2px, no inner border |
| Coloring page | Outer solid 3px, no inner border |
| Answer key | Outer solid 2px + inner dashed 1px |

## 8.4 Forbidden Border Practices

- FORBIDDEN: Drop shadows on borders (poor print quality)
- FORBIDDEN: Pattern fills that consume space
- FORBIDDEN: Borders with illustrations embedded
- FORBIDDEN: Multiple border weights (only the two-layer system)
- FORBIDDEN: Sharp corners — all borders must use border-radius >= 8px
- FORBIDDEN: Coloured borders in B&W format

## 8.5 Activity Row Card Borders

| Specification | Value |
|---------------|-------|
| Border weight | 2px solid |
| Border colour | #1a1a1a |
| Border radius | 8–9px |
| Padding inside | 2mm vertical, 3mm horizontal |

## 8.6 Info Strip Borders

| Specification | Value |
|---------------|-------|
| Border weight | 2.5px solid |
| Border colour | #1a1a1a |
| Border radius | 9px |

# CHAPTER 09 — ILLUSTRATION STYLE GUIDE

## 9.1 Illustration Philosophy

Worksheet Wonder illustrations are **functional storytelling tools**. They do not merely decorate. Every illustration either provides context for the activity, serves as the activity itself, or acts as a navigation aid. Decoration-only illustration is a waste of space and must be eliminated.

## 9.2 Art Style Parameters

### Style Descriptor
- Clean, rounded, expressive, minimal
- Reference aesthetic: between Pixar concept art and a quality children's picture book
- Complexity: Simple enough for a child to recognise immediately; detailed enough to be engaging
- Line weight: Consistent 2–2.5px clean outlines

### Character Design Rules
- All characters must have **rounded forms** — no sharp angles on loveable characters
- Facial expressions must be **clearly readable**
- Eyes: Simple dots or small circles
- Mouths: Simple curves — smile, neutral, surprised
- All characters should appear **child-proportioned** (larger heads, shorter limbs)
- Human characters should represent **diverse ethnicities** when included

### Object Illustration Rules
- All objects must be immediately recognisable from their silhouette
- Objects should be slightly stylised — not photorealistic, not overly abstract
- Internal detail is suggested rather than exhaustively drawn

## 9.3 Illustration in B&W Format

- Fill areas with white (#fff) or light grey (#f0f0f0 to #f8f8f8) only
- Differentiate areas using stroke patterns (dashed, dotted) rather than fills
- Use hatching or dot textures sparingly for shadow/depth
- All illustrations must read clearly when printed on standard 80gsm white paper

## 9.4 Illustration Themes by Subject

| Subject | Approved Themes |
|---------|----------------|
| Pre-writing | Trains, roads, rivers, animals, weather, plants, food |
| Phonics | Animals (alliterative), everyday objects, nature |
| Numeracy | Food, animals, vehicles, sport, nature |
| Science | Plants, weather, animals, solar system, simple machines |
| Social-emotional | Children in scenarios, emotions, community helpers |

## 9.5 Forbidden Illustration Content

- FORBIDDEN: Violent or frightening imagery
- FORBIDDEN: Gender stereotypes in role depictions
- FORBIDDEN: Culturally exclusive imagery
- FORBIDDEN: Trademarked characters
- FORBIDDEN: Photographic imagery (illustration only)
- FORBIDDEN: AI-generated art that appears repetitive or uncanny
- FORBIDDEN: Illustrations copied from other publishers

## 9.6 SVG vs Raster Illustrations

**Preferred format: SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics)**
- Resolution-independent — perfect at any print size
- Editable — can be modified without degradation
- Accessible — can include aria-label attributes

**When raster (PNG/JPEG) is permitted:**
- Complex photographic textures used as subtle backgrounds
- Pre-existing approved assets
- Always minimum 300 DPI at intended print size

## 9.7 Illustration Placement Hierarchy

1. Activity-serving illustrations (highest priority) — in the activity zone
2. Context-setting illustrations (second priority) — in the title block
3. Decorative illustrations (lowest priority) — only if space permits

# CHAPTER 10 — MASCOT DESIGN GUIDE

## 10.1 The Role of a Mascot

A recurring mascot is one of the most powerful tools in children's educational design. # CHAPTER 12 — LINE THICKNESS STANDARDS

## 12.1 Why Line Thickness Matters

In educational design for children, line thickness is a **functional specification** tied directly to the child's developmental motor stage. Too thin: the child cannot see the line clearly. Too thick: the line overwhelms the space.

## 12.2 Line Thickness Reference Table

| Line Function | Age 2–3 | Age 3–5 | Age 5–7 | Age 7–10 |
|---------------|---------|---------|---------|----------|
| Tracing guide (dashed) | 6–7px | 4.5–5px | 3.5–4px | 2.5–3px |
| Writing baseline | 1.5px | 1.5px | 1px | 0.75px |
| Page border outer | 3px | 3px | 2.5px | 2.5px |
| Page border inner | 1.5px | 1.5px | 1.5px | 1px |
| Activity row card border | 2px | 2px | 2px | 1.5px |
| Illustration outline | 2.5px | 2–2.5px | 1.5–2px | 1.5px |
| Illustration interior | 1.5px | 1–1.5px | 1–1.5px | 1px |
| Divider lines | 1.5px | 1.5px | 1.5px | 1px |
| Start/end dot radius | 7px | 6px | 5px | 4px |
| End circle radius | 7px | 6px | 5px | 4px |
| End circle stroke | 2.5px | 2.5px | 2px | 1.5px |

## 12.3 Illustration Line Weight Hierarchy

| Line Type | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Primary character outline | 2.5px |
| Secondary character detail | 1.5px |
| Tertiary detail / texture | 1px |
| Decorative/background elements | 0.75–1px |
| Watermark / ghost guides | 0.5px |

Never allow detail lines to be heavier than primary outline lines within the same illustration.

# CHAPTER 13 — TRACING LINE STANDARDS

## 13.1 Tracing as a Pedagogical Tool

Tracing is the entry point for all fine motor and pre-writing skill development. The design of tracing lines directly affects whether the child can see and follow the path, whether success is achievable within their current motor capability, and whether the activity builds confidence or frustration.

## 13.2 Tracing Line Anatomy

Every tracing line path consists of:

```
[Start Dot - filled, 7px radius]  [Dashed guide - 4.5px stroke]  [End Circle - hollow, 7px, 2.5px stroke]
```

Optional elements:
- Direction arrow embedded in or adjacent to the dashed line
- "start" / "end" label in 8pt Nunito 700, colour #999, positioned below the dot/circle
- Intermediate waypoints (for complex paths)

## 13.3 Tracing Line Types

| Line Type | SVG Stroke Attributes | Use |
|-----------|----------------------|-----|
| Straight dashed | stroke-dasharray="13 8" | All straight-line tracing |
| Curve dashed | stroke-dasharray="10 7" | Gently curved paths |
| Complex curve | stroke-dasharray="8 6" | S-curves, spirals |
| Ghost guide | stroke-dasharray="4 4" opacity="0.4" | Reducing-scaffold activities |
| Solid example | No dasharray | First row of any activity (model row) |

## 13.4 Tracing Difficulty Calibration

| Difficulty Level | Line Characteristics |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Easy (1 star) | Long, single-direction, well-spaced dashes, large start/end markers |
| Medium (2 stars) | Shorter dashes, direction changes, reduced start/end markers |
| Hard (3 stars) | Minimal dashes, complex paths, small markers, less whitespace around path |

## 13.5 Path Spacing Requirements

The clear space around any tracing path must not be less than:

| Age Group | Minimum Clear Space Around Path |
|-----------|---------------------------------|
| 2–3 years | 12mm |
| 3–4 years | 10mm |
| 4–5 years | 8mm |
| 5–7 years | 6mm |
| 7–10 years | 4mm |

## 13.6 Tracing Line Colour in B&W

| Element | Colour Value |
|---------|-------------|
| Dashed guide line | #555 (medium-dark grey) |
| Start dot | #1a1a1a (near-black) |
| End circle stroke | #1a1a1a (near-black) |
| Direction arrow | #555 (matches guide) |
| Labels ("start"/"end") | #999 (light grey) |
| Decorative rail/track | #aaa–#ccc |

## 13.7 Tracing Illustration Integration

When tracing is integrated into a scene:
- The tracing path must be on a visually clear background (white or near-white)
- Illustrations must be positioned so they do not intersect with the tracing zone
- Start and end illustrations must be scaled so the path is the visual focal point
- The path must occupy at least 60% of the horizontal width of the trace area

# CHAPTER 23 — DYSLEXIA-FRIENDLY DESIGN

## 23.1 Understanding Dyslexia in Educational Materials

Dyslexia affects 10–15% of the population. Many children using Worksheet Wonder materials will have dyslexia, whether or not formally identified.

## 23.2 Typography Adaptations

| Standard Design | Dyslexia-Friendly Adaptation |
|-----------------|------------------------------|
| Nunito (rounded) | Already appropriate — rounded letterforms reduce confusion |
| Justify text | Never justified — always left-aligned |
| Tight line spacing | Minimum 1.5x line height always enforced |
| Long paragraphs | Maximum 3 lines of instruction per block |

## 23.3 Layout Adaptations

- Never allow text to sit on a grey or tinted background
- Generous margin between lines
- Clear visual anchors — row numbers, icons, and labels help navigation
- Predictable structure — same layout pattern on every page

## 23.4 Dyslexia-Specific Bundle Considerations

When producing resources specifically for dyslexia support:
- Use OpenDyslexic or Dyslexie font as the body typeface
- Increase line height to 2.0x
- Use cream/warm-white paper recommendation (reduces visual stress)
- Include coloured overlay suggestions in teacher/parent guide
- Reduce words per line to maximum 10

## 23.5 Content Adaptations

- All instructions available in numbered bullet points (not prose)
- Key words bolded in body text
- No spelling traps in instruction language
- Phonetically regular words preferred in phonics content

# CHAPTER 24 — ADHD-FRIENDLY DESIGN

## 24.1 Understanding ADHD in the Classroom

Children with ADHD experience challenges with sustained attention, impulse control, and task management. Worksheet design can either exacerbate these challenges or mitigate them.

## 24.2 Design Principles for ADHD

| Challenge | Design Response |
|-----------|----------------|
| Difficulty sustaining attention | Short, clearly segmented activities (chunked rows) |
| Impulsivity (rushing through) | Clear "stop" indicators at end of each section |
| Distractibility | Minimal decorative elements in the activity zone |
| Working memory load | All instructions visible on the same page (no page turns mid-task) |
| Organisation challenges | Numbered steps, clear visual sequence |
| Time management | Estimated time on teacher/parent guide |

## 24.3 Layout Modifications

- Clear visual boundaries between each activity — row cards with visible borders
- Progress indicators built into multi-step activities
- Short activities — maximum 8 rows per page
- Predictable structure — ADHD learners function better with consistent page architecture
- No busy backgrounds or heavily decorated activity zones

## 24.4 Engagement Hooks

- Theme every page around a narrative
- Use a consistent mascot as an anchor
- Include a simple "brain break" suggestion in teacher/parent guides
- Celebrate partial completion — reward stickers for each completed row

## 24.5 Movement Integration

Teacher guides for ADHD-inclusive resources must include a movement break suggestion for every activity phase:
- Pre-activity: gross motor warm-up
- Mid-activity: stand and trace in the air
- Post-activity: celebration dance/movement

# CHAPTER 25 — AUTISM-FRIENDLY CONSIDERATIONS

## 25.1 Designing for Autistic Learners

Our goal is not to design a single "autism worksheet" but to ensure our standard worksheets do not create unnecessary barriers for autistic learners.

## 25.2 Sensory Considerations (Print)

- No busy backgrounds — white page only (reduces sensory overload)
- Consistent, predictable layouts — autistic learners often rely on routine
- No sudden colour changes between elements
- Cream paper recommendation in guides for light-sensitive learners

## 25.3 Instruction Clarity

- Literal, unambiguous language — avoid idioms in child-facing text
- Visual + text instructions — never text-only
- One instruction per line — never combine two steps in one sentence
- Example always provided — never assume prior understanding

## 25.4 Social Scenarios

- Avoid social inference tasks unless explicitly teaching social understanding
- Emotion illustrations must be explicit and clear
- Support scripts included in teacher guide

## 25.5 Special Interest Integration

Teacher guides note that worksheets can be adapted to incorporate a child's special interest:
- "If your child loves trains, use WS 01 and narrate the train's journey in detail"
- "Allow themed substitution — dinosaurs instead of the provided theme"

- "I can trace a horizontal line from left to right without lifting my pencil."
- "I can identify the initial sound in a three-letter word."
- "I can count objects in a set of up to ten."

## 29.2 Outcome Quality Criteria

| Criterion | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| Observable | Can be seen or measured — not "understand" or "appreciate" |
| Specific | Refers to one exact skill, not a broad domain |
| Achievable | Possible for the target age/stage within one session |
| Aligned | Matches the actual activity on the page |

## 29.3 Outcome Display on Worksheet

- Written in first-person child voice ("I can...")
- Maximum 2 lines at 8pt
- Font: Nunito 700
- Location: Info strip, block titled with the Learning Objective label

## 29.4 Bundle-Level Outcomes

Each bundle publishes 8–10 bundle-level outcomes in: the Teacher Guide, product descriptions, and the bundle cover page (condensed version).

# CHAPTER 31 — DIFFICULTY PROGRESSION RULES

## 31.1 The Three-Phase System

Every Worksheet Wonder bundle is organised into exactly three difficulty phases:

| Phase | Symbol | Name | Description |
|-------|--------|------|-------------|
| 1 | 1 star | Easy / Foundation | Core skill introduced with maximum scaffolding |
| 2 | 2 stars | Medium / Developing | Skill extended with reduced support |
| 3 | 3 stars | Hard / Mastery | Full independence, complex application |

## 31.2 Phase Distribution

| Bundle Size | Phase 1 | Phase 2 | Phase 3 |
|-------------|---------|---------|---------|
| 10 worksheets | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| 15 worksheets | 4 | 6 | 5 |
| 20 worksheets | 5 | 8 | 7 |
| 25 worksheets | 6 | 9 | 10 |
| 30 worksheets | 7 | 12 | 11 |

## 31.3 Within-Phase Progression

Within each phase, difficulty still progresses. The first worksheet of Phase 1 is simpler than the last worksheet of Phase 1. Progression is never flat.

## 31.4 Scaffolding Reduction Schedule

| Within-Phase Position | Scaffolding Level |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| First 30% | Maximum scaffolding |
| Middle 40% | Moderate scaffolding |
| Final 30% | Minimum scaffolding (preparation for next phase) |

## 31.5 Regression Prohibition

No worksheet in a later phase may be easier than a worksheet in an earlier phase. Difficulty only moves forward within a bundle.
