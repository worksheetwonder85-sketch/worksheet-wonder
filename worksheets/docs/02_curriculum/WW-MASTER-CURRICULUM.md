### Reading the Curriculum Entry

Each grade-level subject entry contains:

1. **Subject Overview** — What is taught and why
2. **Units Table** — Units, topics, estimated worksheet count
3. **Bundle Names** — Official product names using WW naming convention
4. **Learning Outcomes** — Numbered, measurable "I can" statements
5. **Difficulty Progression** — Three phases (Foundation / Developing / Mastery)
6. **Estimated Worksheet Count** — Per unit and per subject
7. **Suggested Activities** — Hands-on, multisensory extensions
8. **Teacher Resources** — Guides, posters, tools included in bundles

### SUBJECT 1 — PRE-WRITING SKILLS

**Overview:** Pre-writing skills are the physical and cognitive foundation for all future handwriting. At this stage, children develop pencil grip, hand-eye coordination, left-to-right directionality, and the ability to follow a guided path. Every activity is themed to make tracing feel like an adventure.

#### Units Overview

| Unit | Unit Name | Topics | Est. Worksheets |
|------|-----------|--------|----------------|
| PWS-P-1 | Tracing Straight Lines | Horizontal, vertical, diagonal, crossing lines | 20–25 |
| PWS-P-2 | Tracing Curved Lines | Arches, waves, spirals, S-curves | 20–25 |
| PWS-P-3 | Tracing Mixed Lines | Combined straight & curved, zigzag, paths | 15–20 |
| PWS-P-4 | Pre-Shape Tracing | Circles, squares, triangles, rectangles | 15–20 |
| PWS-P-5 | Tracing Patterns | Repeating line patterns, borders | 10–15 |
| PWS-P-6 | Pre-Letter Strokes | Component strokes for letter groups | 10–15 |

---

#### UNIT PWS-P-1: Tracing Straight Lines

**Topics & Subtopics:**

| Topic | Subtopics |
|-------|-----------|
| Horizontal Lines | Short lines, long lines, two-segment lines, themed scenes (trains, bridges, roads) |
| Vertical Lines | Short columns, tall lines, fence posts, rulers, ladders |
| Diagonal Lines | Downward-right, downward-left, rain, slides, ramps |
| Crossing Lines | Intersecting horizontals and verticals, grid practice, plus signs |
| Mixed Straight Directions | Changing direction within one path, L-shapes, T-shapes |

**Bundle Names:**

- `WW Preschool Pre-Writing: Tracing Horizontal Lines — Book 1`
- `WW Preschool Pre-Writing: Tracing Vertical Lines — Book 1`
- `WW Preschool Pre-Writing: Tracing Diagonal Lines — Book 1`
- `WW Preschool Pre-Writing: Straight Lines Complete Bundle`

**Learning Outcomes:**

1. 🎯 I can move my pencil from left to right to complete a horizontal line.
2. 🎯 I can move my pencil from top to bottom to complete a vertical line.
3. 🎯 I can trace a diagonal line from top-left to bottom-right.
4. 🎯 I can keep my pencil on the dashed guide line.
5. 🎯 I can start at the dot and stop at the circle.
6. 🎯 I can trace a line without lifting my pencil off the page.
7. 🎯 I can trace lines of different lengths.

**Difficulty Progression:**

| Phase | Description | Activities |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| ⭐ Foundation | Single short lines with maximum dashes and large start/end markers | WS 1–8 |
| ⭐⭐ Developing | Longer lines, two-segment lines, direction changes | WS 9–16 |
| ⭐⭐⭐ Mastery | Multi-line scenes, reduced guide marks, themed full-page activities | WS 17–25 |

**Estimated Worksheet Count:** 20–25 worksheets

**Suggested Activities:**
- 🧩 Trace horizontal lines in sand tray before attempting paper
- 🧩 "Train on the track" — child narrates a train journey while tracing
- 🧩 Air tracing — large arm movements before fine pencil work
- 🧩 Washable paint on foil — gross motor straight line practice
- 🧩 Masking tape lines on floor — child "drives" toy car along straight path
- 🧩 Finger tracing on textured surfaces (corrugated card, sandpaper)
- 🧩 Paired activity: one child draws start dot, other child traces to it

**Teacher Resources:**
- 📋 Teacher guide with pencil grip photography guide (3-step poster)
- 📋 Differentiation table (emerging / developing / extending)
- 📋 Printable pencil grip observation checklist
- 📋 Parent letter explaining pre-writing importance
- 📋 Lamination guide for reusable practice sheets
- 📋 Classroom display: "We are learning to trace!" poster

**Assessment Ideas:**
- ✅ Teacher observation checklist: Does child maintain grip? Stay on the line? Complete the path?
- ✅ Portfolio: dated collection of Week 1 vs Week 8 tracing samples
- ✅ Self-assessment: "I traced the line today!" smiley tick box at bottom of sheet
- ✅ Parent report: "What I notice at home about my child's pencil control"

### SUBJECT 2 — PHONOLOGICAL AWARENESS

**Overview:** Phonological awareness is the ability to hear, identify, and manipulate units of spoken language — words, syllables, rhymes, and phonemes. At the preschool level, all work is oral and auditory, supported by visual worksheets that prompt, record, and celebrate listening and sound play activities.

#### Units Overview

| Unit | Unit Name | Topics | Est. Worksheets |
|------|-----------|--------|----------------|
| PA-P-1 | Listening & Sound Recognition | Environmental sounds, animal sounds, loud/soft | 10–12 |
| PA-P-2 | Rhyme & Rhythm | Nursery rhymes, rhyming pairs, rhyme completion | 15–18 |
| PA-P-3 | Syllables | Clapping syllables, syllable counting, word pictures | 12–15 |
| PA-P-4 | Beginning Sounds | Initial sound identification, alliteration, sound sorting | 15–18 |
| PA-P-5 | Word Awareness | Sentences, word boundaries, long vs short words | 10–12 |

---

#### UNIT PA-P-1: Listening & Sound Recognition

**Topics & Subtopics:**

| Topic | Subtopics |
|-------|-----------|
| Environmental Sounds | Identifying sounds in pictures (bell, drum, water), draw what you hear |
| Animal Sounds | Match animal to sound, what sound does it make?, farm/jungle/ocean sounds |
| Loud and Soft | Sorting sounds by volume, quiet vs loud objects, classroom sounds |
| Same and Different Sounds | Do these sound the same?, odd one out sound games |

**Bundle Names:**

- `WW Preschool Phonological Awareness: Listening & Sound Skills`
- `WW Preschool Phonological Awareness: Animal Sounds & Sorting`

**Learning Outcomes:**

1. 🎯 I can listen carefully and identify familiar sounds.
2. 🎯 I can match an animal to the sound it makes.
3. 🎯 I can sort sounds into loud and soft.
4. 🎯 I can tell if two sounds are the same or different.
5. 🎯 I can draw something that makes a particular sound.

**Difficulty Progression:**

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| ⭐ Foundation | 3-picture match activities; circle the correct animal |
| ⭐⭐ Developing | Sort into two categories; odd one out |
| ⭐⭐⭐ Mastery | Draw your own + label; explain why to a friend |

**Estimated Worksheet Count:** 10–12 worksheets

**Suggested Activities:**
- 🧩 Sound walk around school — draw 5 sounds you hear
- 🧩 "Mystery bag" — guess what's inside by shaking it
- 🧩 Sorting cards: loud things vs quiet things (match to worksheet)
- 🧩 Sound map: draw the classroom and mark where each sound comes from

**Teacher Resources:**
- 📋 Listening activity cards (paired with worksheets)
- 📋 Sound vocabulary word wall cards
- 📋 Parent guide: "Listening games you can play at home"

**Assessment Ideas:**
- ✅ Observation during sound walk
- ✅ Sorting accuracy on worksheet (teacher ticks observed)

### SUBJECT 3 — PRE-MATHEMATICS

**Overview:** Pre-mathematics at the preschool level builds the conceptual foundations that all formal number work rests upon: understanding quantity, recognising patterns, exploring shape, and developing spatial reasoning. All activities use concrete-pictorial representations before abstract notation.

#### Units Overview

| Unit | Unit Name | Topics | Est. Worksheets |
|------|-----------|--------|----------------|
| PM-P-1 | Numbers 1–5 | Counting, recognition, quantity matching | 15–20 |
| PM-P-2 | Numbers 6–10 | Extending counting, subitising, comparison | 15–20 |
| PM-P-3 | Shapes & Geometry | 2D shapes, sorting, shape in environment | 15–18 |
| PM-P-4 | Patterns | AB, ABB, ABC patterns; create and extend | 12–15 |
| PM-P-5 | Measurement Concepts | Big/small, heavy/light, full/empty, long/short | 10–12 |
| PM-P-6 | Sorting & Classifying | Sort by colour, size, shape, texture | 10–12 |

---

#### UNIT PM-P-1: Numbers 1–5

**Topics & Subtopics:**

| Topic | Subtopics |
|-------|-----------|
| Number Recognition 1–5 | Identify written numerals, trace numerals, match numeral to word |
| Counting 1–5 | Count objects in a picture, point and count, one-to-one correspondence |
| Quantity Matching | Match number to set of objects, draw objects to match numeral |
| Number Order | Fill in missing number, which comes next?, 1-to-5 number lines |
| More and Fewer (1–5) | Which group has more?, which has fewer?, making sets equal |

**Bundle Names:**

- `WW Preschool Mathematics: Counting & Numbers 1–5`
- `WW Preschool Mathematics: Number Recognition 1–5 Tracing`
- `WW Preschool Mathematics: Matching Numbers & Quantities 1–5`

**Learning Outcomes:**

1. 🎯 I can count objects in a group of up to 5.
2. 🎯 I can recognise written numerals 1–5.
3. 🎯 I can match a numeral to the correct number of objects.
4. 🎯 I can draw the correct number of objects for a given numeral.
5. 🎯 I can order the numbers 1–5 correctly.
6. 🎯 I can say which group has more and which has fewer (up to 5).
7. 🎯 I can identify the number that comes before and after a given number (1–5).

**Difficulty Progression:**

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| ⭐ Foundation | Trace the numeral; count and circle the right number of pictures (up to 3) |
| ⭐⭐ Developing | Match numeral to set; fill in missing number on a line (up to 5) |
| ⭐⭐⭐ Mastery | Draw your own set; compare two groups; write the numeral independently |

**Estimated Worksheet Count:** 15–20 worksheets

**Suggested Activities:**
- 🧩 Five-frame stamping — stamp the correct number of dots
- 🧩 Counting bears on numbered mats (match to worksheet number)
- 🧩 "Show me 3!" — children hold up fingers, then find it on worksheet
- 🧩 Numeral tracing in sand before tracing on paper
- 🧩 Giant number hopscotch — jump to the number called out
- 🧩 Counting snack — count crackers/raisins to match the day's number

**Teacher Resources:**
- 📋 Number posters 1–5 (with objects + numeral + word)
- 📋 Five-frame printable mat for hands-on counting companion
- 📋 Parent counting activity cards (10 home games)
- 📋 Differentiation guide: using manipulatives alongside worksheets

**Assessment Ideas:**
- ✅ One-to-one correspondence check: Does child touch each object once while counting?
- ✅ Numeral recognition flash card check (1–5)
- ✅ Portfolio: independent numeral drawing at start and end of unit
- ✅ Observation: Can child self-correct when a count goes wrong?

### SUBJECT 4 — SCIENCE DISCOVERY

**Overview:** Preschool science is entirely inquiry-based and observation-led. Worksheets support children in recording, drawing, and discussing what they notice about the natural and physical world. All topics align with early childhood science frameworks (NGSS Early Learning progressions, EYFS Understanding the World).

#### Units Overview

| Unit | Unit Name | Topics | Est. Worksheets |
|------|-----------|--------|----------------|

| SC-P-3 | My Senses | Five senses, sense matching, observation recording | 12–15 |
| SC-P-4 | Materials & Properties | Hard/soft, rough/smooth, floating/sinking | 10–12 |

---

#### UNIT SC-P-1: Living Things

**Topics & Subtopics:**

| Topic | Subtopics |
|-------|-----------|
| Plants | Parts of a plant (leaf, root, stem, flower), what plants need (sun, water, soil), plant observation diary |
| Animals | Animal homes, baby animals and parents, animal coverings (fur, feathers, scales) |
| Life Cycles | Butterfly (egg-caterpillar-chrysalis-butterfly), frog (egg-tadpole-frog), simple plant cycle |
| Needs of Living Things | Food, water, shelter, air — sorting what living things need |

**Bundle Names:**

- `WW Preschool Science: Plants & What They Need`
- `WW Preschool Science: Animals & Their Homes`
- `WW Preschool Science: Simple Life Cycles — Butterflies & Frogs`
- `WW Preschool Science: Living & Non-Living Things`

**Learning Outcomes:**

1. 🎯 I can name the main parts of a plant (leaf, stem, root, flower).
2. 🎯 I can say what a plant needs to grow.
3. 🎯 I can match baby animals to their parents.
4. 🎯 I can name the four stages of a butterfly's life cycle.
5. 🎯 I can sort pictures into living and non-living things.
6. 🎯 I can draw and label a simple observation of a plant or animal.

**Difficulty Progression:**

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| ⭐ Foundation | Colour the plant parts; circle what the animal needs |
| ⭐⭐ Developing | Label plant diagram; sequence life cycle cards |
| ⭐⭐⭐ Mastery | Draw and describe a living thing; sort and explain reasoning |

**Estimated Worksheet Count:** 15–18 worksheets

**Suggested Activities:**

- 🧩 Animal homes matching game (companion to worksheet)
- 🧩 Lifecycle wheel craft alongside sequencing worksheet
- 🧩 Nature walk: collect and draw leaves, seeds, and insects observed
- 🧩 "This is alive because..." — sentence starter discussion
- 🧩 Butterfly garden observation journal

**Teacher Resources:**
- 📋 Full-colour plant diagram poster (companion B&W worksheet version)
- 📋 Life cycle poster set (butterfly, frog, plant)
- 📋 Observation journal template for ongoing science recording
- 📋 Parent science extension letter: "Growing a bean at home"

**Assessment Ideas:**
- ✅ Plant part labelling check (oral + pointing)
- ✅ Life cycle sequencing: can child put cards in correct order?
- ✅ Living/non-living sort: 10-item accuracy check
- ✅ Science journal portfolio: weekly plant observation comparison
