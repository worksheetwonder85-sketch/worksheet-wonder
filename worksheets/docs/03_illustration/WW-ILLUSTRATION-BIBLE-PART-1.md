# CHAPTER 01 — ILLUSTRATION PHILOSOPHY

## 1.1 The Studio Mandate

Worksheet Wonder illustrations are not clip art. They are not stock vectors. They are not the output of a prompt engine fed into an image model and exported without thought. They are **purposefully designed visual narratives** — each one serving a specific pedagogical role, executed in a style so consistent and so warm that a child will recognise it instantly across every product in our catalogue.

Our illustrations sit at the intersection of four creative traditions:

1. **The golden age of children's picture books** — think Jan Brett, Oliver Jeffers, and Quentin Blake. Warmth, personality, and genuine love for the audience.
2. **Pixar's concept art philosophy** — characters with clear silhouettes, readable emotions, and world-class appeal to both children and adults.
3. **Scandinavian children's product design** — clean, minimal, friendly. Never cluttered. Never frightening. Always purposeful.
4. **Classic educational illustration** — encyclopedic clarity, accurate representation, and the ability to communicate complex ideas at a glance.

The result is a style that is instantly recognisable, deeply human, and always, always in service of the child using the page.

## 1.2 The Purpose Hierarchy

Every illustration produced for Worksheet Wonder exists in one of four categories, listed in priority order:

| Priority | Category | Definition |
|----------|----------|------------|
| 1 | **Functional** | The illustration IS the activity (trace this, colour this, count these) |
| 2 | **Contextual** | The illustration provides the narrative frame for the activity |
| 3 | **Navigational** | The illustration directs, points, or signals within the page |
| 4 | **Decorative** | The illustration adds warmth without serving the above — permitted only when space allows |

> **Rule:** If an illustration serves no purpose from the above hierarchy, it must be removed.
> Decoration at the expense of clarity is a design failure.

## 1.3 The Emotional Contract

Our illustrations make a promise to the child who receives the page:

- *"You are safe here."* — Nothing on this page is frightening, threatening, or confusing.
- *"This is going to be fun."* — The image sparks curiosity and play.
- *"You can do this."* — The character in the scene is like you. They are trying. They are succeeding.
- *"You are seen."* — The children in our illustrations look like children everywhere.

This emotional contract is not broken. Not for deadline. Not for convenience. Not ever.

## 1.4 What Separates Good from Extraordinary

The difference between a worksheet illustration and a **Worksheet Wonder** illustration:

| Ordinary | Worksheet Wonder |
|----------|-----------------|
| Generic smiley face | Nuanced expression that matches the page's emotional tone |
| Random clip art animal | A specific animal character with personality, posture, and narrative role |
| Filler background shape | A purposeful scene that frames and enhances the activity |
| Copied internet illustration | An original, hand-crafted SVG path with intentional curves |
| Rushed outline | Confident, deliberate line weight that reads beautifully at 50% scale |

# CHAPTER 02 — CHILD-FRIENDLY DESIGN PRINCIPLES

## 2.1 The Seven Laws of Child-Friendly Illustration

These seven laws are non-negotiable. Every illustration is reviewed against all seven before publication.

### Law 1 — Safety First
Nothing in a Worksheet Wonder illustration causes fear, discomfort, or confusion in a child aged 2–12.

**Permitted:**
- Friendly monsters with big eyes and wide smiles
- Cartoon animals in relatable situations
- Mild cartoon conflict (a character looking surprised or puzzled)

**Forbidden:**
- Sharp-toothed predators in aggressive poses
- Realistic injury, blood, or death (even in nature scenes)
- Darkness, shadows, or isolated figures that read as threatening
- Adult-themed scenarios
- Realistic depictions of danger

### Law 2 — Roundness as Safety Signal
Rounded forms are universally perceived as safe, approachable, and friendly. Sharp forms signal danger. This is not cultural — it is evolutionary.

**Application:**
- All character outlines use rounded corners and soft curves
- No character has sharp, angular features on their primary silhouette
- Even "hard" objects (scissors, tools) are illustrated with softened forms
- Corner radii on geometric elements: minimum 15–20% of the smaller dimension

### Law 3 — Clarity of Silhouette
A child must recognise what they are looking at within 1 second. If the silhouette alone does not communicate the subject, the illustration has failed its first test.

**Test:** Cover all internal detail and colour. Does the outline alone tell you what it is?

### Law 4 — Emotional Legibility
All facial expressions must be universally readable. No ambiguous expressions. No subtle irony. No cultural specificity in expression.

- Happiness: wide smile, raised cheeks, bright eyes
- Sadness: downturned mouth, lowered brow (never used on child characters as a final state)
- Surprise: wide eyes, open mouth, raised eyebrows
- Concentration: slight brow furrow, small focused mouth
- Pride: chest forward, big smile, eyes bright

### Law 5 — Proportional Warmth
Child characters and character proxies (animals, objects) must use child-friendly proportions:
- Head-to-body ratio: 1:2 to 1:3 (large head = vulnerable, safe, endearing)
- Large eyes relative to face (40–50% of face area occupied by eyes + brow)
- Short limbs relative to torso
- Plump, rounded extremities

### Law 6 — Meaningful Activity
Characters in illustrations are always *doing something* — not simply posing. Activity creates narrative. Narrative creates engagement.

**Permitted poses:**
- Running, jumping, waving, pointing, reading, writing, building, exploring, celebrating

**Forbidden poses:**
- Static standing with arms at sides
- Empty staring with no apparent focus
- Lying down (except in specific sleep/rest contexts)

### Law 7 — Diversity by Default
Every set of human characters created for Worksheet Wonder must represent diversity across:
- Skin tone (minimum 4 distinct tones represented across a set)
- Hair type (straight, wavy, curly, coiled — all represented)
- Body shape (not all characters are the same build)
- Ability (characters with glasses, hearing aids, and mobility aids appear naturalistically)

> Diversity is never a checkbox exercise. It is the natural reflection of our global audience.

## 2.2 Age-Calibrated Complexity

The visual complexity of an illustration must match the cognitive stage of the target user:

| Age Range | Max Elements in One Scene | Line Complexity | Character Count per Scene |
|-----------|--------------------------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| 2–3 years | 3 | Thick, minimal | 1 |
| 3–4 years | 5 | Thick, simple | 1–2 |
| 4–5 years | 7 | Medium | 1–3 |
| 5–7 years | 10 | Medium | 2–4 |
| 7–10 years | 15 | Standard | 3–5 |
| 10–12 years | 20 | Standard–detailed | 4–6 |

## 2.3 Visual Noise Prohibition

Visual noise is anything in an illustration that competes with the learning objective without adding value. Common sources:

| Noise Source | How to Eliminate |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Excessive cross-hatching | Use flat tones or simple dot textures only |
| Too many overlapping elements | Separate elements with clear whitespace |
| Competing focal points | One primary focal point per illustration |
| Inconsistent line weights | Follow the line weight system in Chapter 11 |
| Over-decorated backgrounds | Backgrounds are minimal; they frame, they do not compete |
| Perspective distortion | Avoid complex perspective; use isometric or flat front-on views |

# CHAPTER 03 — EDUCATIONAL ILLUSTRATION PRINCIPLES

## 3.1 The Illustration as a Teaching Tool

A Worksheet Wonder illustration is never passive decoration. It is an active component of the learning experience. Before creating any illustration, the creator must answer:

1. **What does the child learn from looking at this?**
2. **Does the illustration make the learning objective clearer or harder?**
3. **Could the activity be completed without this illustration?** (If yes, is the illustration still worth including?)

## 3.2 The Three Functions of Educational Illustration

### Function A — Motivation
The illustration engages the child emotionally and creates a desire to interact with the page. A train approaching a station motivates the child to "complete the track" by tracing. A happy character waving from a finish line motivates completion.

**Design requirement:** The motivating illustration must be clearly separate from the activity zone and must not interfere with the child's physical workspace.

### Function B — Instruction
The illustration communicates how to do the task — often more effectively than words. An arrow showing direction. A hand demonstrating grip. A completed example showing the target outcome.

**Design requirement:** Instructional illustrations must use directional arrows, numbered sequences, or clear before/after contrast. They must be positioned where the eye naturally arrives first.

### Function C — Assessment Support
The illustration provides the stimulus for the child's response — the picture they are labelling, the scene they are comprehending, the object they are counting.

**Design requirement:** Assessment illustrations must be accurate, unambiguous, and at a scale that allows comfortable viewing. They must never accidentally hint at the correct answer beyond what is intended.

## 3.3 Pedagogical Accuracy

For subject-specific illustrations, factual accuracy is mandatory:

| Subject | Accuracy Requirement |
|---------|---------------------|
| Science | Anatomically and scientifically accurate (simplified, not distorted) |
| Mathematics | Geometrically precise (use SVG tools, not freehand estimation) |
| History & Geography | Culturally respectful; historically contextualised |
| Nature | Species correctly represented (a robin looks like a robin) |
| Technology | Contemporary and functional (not retro or fantastical) |

**One exception is permitted:** When cartoonisation of a real subject is required for child engagement, anatomical exaggeration is allowed, provided the subject remains identifiable and no false information is conveyed (e.g., a cartoon frog with large eyes is acceptable; a frog with four hind legs is not).

## 3.4 Left-to-Right Directionality

All narrative illustrations must reinforce left-to-right reading direction:
- Characters face right by default (indicating forward progress)
- Journeys, paths, and sequences progress from left to right
- The "start" of any activity is anchored to the left side of the page
- Exception: cultural adaptation versions may reverse for right-to-left markets

## 3.5 Scale Relationships

When multiple objects appear in the same illustration, their relative scale must be pedagogically honest:
- A dog must be smaller than a car, larger than a mouse
- A mountain must be larger than a house
- A child must be smaller than a tree

Scale distortions that are cartoonishly exaggerated are acceptable only when they serve a clear narrative purpose (e.g., a tiny human next to a giant vegetable for a size-comparison activity).

# CHAPTER 04 — VISUAL LANGUAGE

## 4.1 What Is the Worksheet Wonder Visual Language?

Visual language is the complete vocabulary of marks, shapes, tones, and compositions that — taken together — make a product immediately identifiable as Worksheet Wonder. A child who uses our books at age 3 should, when picking up a new bundle at age 8, feel an immediate sense of recognition and trust. That recognition is the product of a rigorously maintained visual language.

## 4.2 The Four Pillars of the Visual Language

### Pillar 1 — Warmth
Every mark is made with warmth. Lines are not cold or mechanical. Even when drawn with perfect SVG precision, they carry a visual quality of care — through deliberate imperfection in curves, slight variation in rounded corners, and humanised proportions.

**Technical implementation:**
- All character paths use smooth Bézier curves (no polyline characters)
- Corner radii are human, not mathematically perfect (slight asymmetry is acceptable and desirable)
- Stroke joints use `stroke-linejoin="round"` and `stroke-linecap="round"` at all times

### Pillar 2 — Clarity
Every mark communicates one clear thing. No mark is ambiguous. No two elements compete for the same visual territory.

**Technical implementation:**
- Maximum 3 levels of visual hierarchy per illustration
- Primary subject always occupies the highest contrast zone
- Supporting elements are lower contrast or smaller
- Background elements are lightest/smallest

### Pillar 3 — Consistency
Every illustration across the entire Worksheet Wonder catalogue — from Preschool to Grade 8, from tracing sheets to science posters — uses the same visual DNA. Different complexity levels, same genetic code.

**Technical implementation:**
- All illustrations share the same colour palette (per Chapter 16)
- All characters share the same proportion rules (per Chapter 06)
- All line weights follow the same hierarchy (per Chapter 11)
- All facial features follow the same system (per Chapters 07–09)

### Pillar 4 — Joy
The visual language is inherently joyful. This does not mean chaotic or busy. Joy is expressed through:
- Characters in motion and action
- Rounded, bouncy proportions
- Slight exaggeration of positive emotional states
- A sense that something interesting is happening in every scene

## 4.3 What the Visual Language Is NOT

| It is not... | Why |
|-------------|-----|
| Retro/vintage | Ages poorly; excludes global markets |
| Hyper-realistic | Intimidating; inappropriate for young children |
| Ultra-minimal (geometric flat) | Too cold; lacks warmth and narrative |
| Complex painterly | Does not print clearly in B&W |
| Trendy/fashion-forward | Dates quickly; undermines timelessness |
| Edgy or ironic | Inappropriate for the audience; confusing |

## 4.4 Compositional Principles

### Rule of Three
Every illustration scene contains no more than three zones of visual interest:
1. **Primary focal point** — the main subject/character
2. **Secondary context** — the environment or supporting element
3. **Tertiary detail** — subtle texture or background element

### Breathing Room
All illustrations maintain a minimum 10% of their bounding box as empty (undrawn) space. This breathing room prevents the illustration from feeling cluttered and allows the child's eye to rest.

### Grounding
All characters and objects are visually grounded on a surface. Nothing floats arbitrarily. A character stands on a floor plane, a ground line, or within a clearly defined space. Ungrounded elements read as incomplete.

**Exception:** Characters in space or underwater scenes, where floating is contextually correct.

### Staged Depth (Two-Layer Maximum)
Preschool and Junior KG illustrations: one layer only (flat, no depth).
Senior KG and above: maximum two layers (foreground and background). Three-layer compositions are reserved for Grade 4 and above.

# CHAPTER 05 — SHAPE LANGUAGE

## 5.1 Shape Personality Theory

In visual design, shapes carry intrinsic psychological meaning. This is not arbitrary — it reflects how the human visual system has evolved to read the natural environment.

| Shape Type | Psychological Reading | Worksheet Wonder Application |
|------------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| **Circles** | Safe, complete, friendly, infinite | Used for: faces, primary character bodies, reward elements, soft objects |
| **Rounded rectangles** | Organised, contained, stable, friendly | Used for: borders, boxes, labels, frames, pages |
| **Ovals / ellipses** | Organic, natural, living | Used for: animal bodies, plants, food, ground shadows |
| **Squares** | Solid, reliable, structured | Used for: buildings, books, devices, learning tools |
| **Triangles (pointing up)** | Aspiring, alert, energetic | Used for: mountains, rooftops, arrows (rounded tips), party hats |
| **Organic blobs** | Playful, unpredictable, natural | Used for: clouds, speech bubbles, splashes, leaves, amoebae |
| **Stars** | Achievement, specialness, magic | Used for: rewards, ratings, celebration |
| **Hearts** | Love, care, connection | Used for: SEL materials, family themes, kindness content |

## 5.2 The Shape Restriction Rule

Sharp-angle triangles with acute inner angles (below 30°) are never used on character designs or child-facing friendly elements. They may appear only in:
- Scientific/geometric diagrams
- Mathematical content where precision requires them
- Architectural or geographic contexts

## 5.3 Shape Softening Protocol

All geometric shapes used in Worksheet Wonder illustrations are softened:

| Raw Shape | Worksheet Wonder Treatment |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| Square | `rx="8"` minimum border radius |
| Rectangle | `rx="6"` minimum border radius |
| Triangle | Rounded tip: `stroke-linejoin="round"` + thick stroke; or explicit Bézier curve replacement |
| Star | All inner and outer points have a radius of ≥ 2px |
| Arrow | Rounded arrowhead; `stroke-linecap="round"` on shaft |
| Diamond | `rx="4"` on all sides |

## 5.4 Shape Grammar by Subject

The dominant shapes used in each subject area create a visual grammar that signals the subject before the child reads a single word:

| Subject | Dominant Shapes | Why |
|---------|-----------------|-----|
| Mathematics | Circles, squares, triangles, grids | Geometric precision |
| Nature / Science | Organic blobs, spirals, branching curves | Natural forms |
| Language / Literacy | Flowing curves, speech bubbles, book rectangles | Communication, language |
| Transport | Ovals, rectangles, wheels (circles) | Mechanical, reliable |
| Fantasy | Stars, clouds, spirals, irregular organic | Magic, imagination |
| Sports | Circles, arcs, dynamic diagonals | Energy, movement |
| Ocean | Waves (sinusoidal), fish silhouettes, bubbles | Fluidity |

## 5.5 Shape Consistency Across Scales

The same shape language must remain consistent whether an illustration is:
- 20mm × 20mm (icon/badge size)
- 60mm × 60mm (activity illustration size)
- Full-page (cover or poster size)

A cloud drawn at icon size must look like the same cloud drawn at full-page size — just scaled, with the same curves, same proportions, same personality.

**Technical enforcement:** All Worksheet Wonder shapes are built in SVG using `viewBox` coordinates with a standard base grid. They scale without distortion because they are inherently resolution-independent. Never rasterise a shape before embedding.

# CHAPTER 06 — CHARACTER PROPORTIONS

## 6.1 The Worksheet Wonder Character Standard

All human and humanoid characters in the Worksheet Wonder system are built on a unified proportion grid. This ensures that a child character drawn for a Preschool tracing sheet and a child character drawn for a Grade 6 science worksheet feel like they inhabit the same world.

## 6.2 The 8-Unit Head System

The Worksheet Wonder character proportion system is based on an **8-unit head** — the character's head height is defined as 1 unit, and all other proportions derive from that.

### Child Character Proportions (Ages 3–8 Representation)

```
HEAD:         1.0 units (the standard unit)
NECK:         0.15 units
TORSO:        1.2 units
UPPER ARM:    0.7 units
LOWER ARM:    0.6 units
HAND:         0.25 units
UPPER LEG:    0.8 units
LOWER LEG:    0.7 units
FOOT:         0.2 units
─────────────────────────
TOTAL HEIGHT: ~5.4 units (head:body ratio = 1:4.4)
SHOULDER WIDTH: 0.9 units
HIP WIDTH:      0.7 units
```

**Result:** A slightly large-headed, compact, endearing child figure. Not realistic. Not caricature. The sweet spot.

### Child Character Proportions (Ages 8–12 Representation)

```
HEAD:         1.0 units
NECK:         0.2 units
TORSO:        1.5 units
UPPER ARM:    0.85 units
LOWER ARM:    0.75 units
HAND:         0.3 units
UPPER LEG:    1.0 units
LOWER LEG:    0.85 units
FOOT:         0.25 units
─────────────────────────
TOTAL HEIGHT: ~6.7 units (head:body ratio = 1:5.7)
SHOULDER WIDTH: 1.1 units
HIP WIDTH:      0.85 units
```

**Result:** A slightly more elongated figure appropriate for older children, but still warmly proportioned.

### Adult Character Proportions (Parent / Teacher)

```
HEAD:         1.0 units
TOTAL HEIGHT: 7.0–7.5 units (head:body ratio = 1:6.5–6.5)
SHOULDER WIDTH: 1.4 units (adults read as larger, more authoritative)
```

Adults are always noticeably larger than child characters in the same scene, reinforcing the natural authority gradient without making adults intimidating.

### Animal Character Proportions (Anthropomorphic)

All anthropomorphic animal characters (standing upright, with expressive faces) use child-character proportions, not realistic animal proportions. This is intentional: it makes them relatable, familiar, and emotionally readable.

| Proportion | Anthropomorphic Animal |
|------------|----------------------|
| Head | 1.2 units (slightly larger than human equivalent) |
| Eyes | 30–40% of face area |
| Total height | 4.5–5.5 units |
| Limb length | Slightly shorter than human for endearing quality |

## 6.3 The Head Anatomy Standard

All character heads follow this internal proportion system:

```
HEAD WIDTH:           1.0 unit (reference)
HEAD HEIGHT:          0.9 units (slightly taller than wide = friendly)
EYE LINE:             at 45% from top of head
NOSE POSITION:        at 65% from top (minimal nose or none)
MOUTH POSITION:       at 78% from top
EAR POSITION:         at eye line (when visible)
CHIN CURVE RADIUS:    20% of head width
CROWN CURVE RADIUS:   55% of head width
```

## 6.4 Body Weight and Volume

Worksheet Wonder characters are **not** thin, stylised, fashion-figure bodies. They have warmth and volume:
- Torsos have gentle barrel curves
- Cheeks are full and rounded
- Limbs have slight tapering but maintain plump volume throughout
- No visible skeletal structure in character illustration
- No defined musculature (except in specific Grade 6–8 science content)

## 6.5 Posture as Personality

A character's posture communicates their emotional state and personality. The following posture vocabulary is standardised:

| Posture | Meaning | Typical Use |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| Chest forward, arms wide | Confident, proud | Reward sections, achievement moments |
| Body tilted forward, one arm pointing | Excited, engaged, guiding | Instruction pointers, activity introductions |
| One foot forward, arms mid-swing | Mid-stride, active, purposeful | Journey themes, progress scenes |
| Head tilted 15°, one brow raised | Curious, questioning | Problem-solving, discovery activities |
| Both arms raised, knees slightly bent | Celebrating | Completion, certificate, achievement |
| One hand on chin, body slightly back | Thinking, considering | Difficult questions, analytical tasks |
| Hands clasped in front, slight forward lean | Welcoming, inviting | Teacher characters, introduction pages |

# CHAPTER 07 — FACIAL EXPRESSION STANDARDS

## 7.1 The Emotional Vocabulary

Worksheet Wonder illustrations use a defined vocabulary of nine primary facial expressions. Every expression used in any illustration must map to one of these nine. New expression types require Art Director approval before creation.

| # | Expression Name | Context | Emotional Signal |
|---|----------------|---------|-----------------|
| 01 | **Happy (Default)** | Standard worksheet appearance | Safe, welcoming, positive |
| 02 | **Excited** | New topic introduction, celebration | High energy, enthusiasm |
| 03 | **Proud** | Completion, achievement, reward section | Accomplishment, self-worth |
| 04 | **Curious** | Problem-solving, discovery | Intellectual engagement |
| 05 | **Concentrating** | Mid-activity, focused work | Appropriate effort |
| 06 | **Surprised** | Discovery, "aha!" moment | Delight, wonder |
| 07 | **Encouraging** | Pointing to start of activity, mid-page prompts | Support, reassurance |
| 08 | **Thinking** | Assessment, reflection, "which one?" tasks | Cognitive engagement |
| 09 | **Welcoming** | Cover pages, teacher characters, guide pages | Trust, invitation |

## 7.2 The Nine Expressions — Technical Specifications

### 01 — Happy (Default)

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Circular, fully open; pupil centred; single white highlight dot at 10–11 o'clock position |
| Brow | Neutral; sits 2–3px above eye; slight arch at outer third |
| Mouth | Simple upward arc; `stroke-linecap="round"`; no teeth visible in preschool characters |
| Cheek | Optional small circle (r=3–5px) in light grey, lower outer eye quadrant |
| Overall | Symmetrical; reads as calm happiness |

### 02 — Excited

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Larger than default (scale up 20%); pupils enlarged; multiple small highlight dots |
| Brow | Raised 4–5px higher than default; arched prominently |
| Mouth | Wide arc (wider than head width × 0.4); teeth permitted from Senior KG upward (2–4 visible) |
| Cheek | Two flush circles (light grey/pink tint in colour version) |
| Overall | Slightly asymmetrical permitted; slight upward tilt of head adds energy |

### 03 — Proud

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Fully open; pupils slightly enlarged; highlight bright |
| Brow | Raised and gently arched; more upward than excited |
| Mouth | Wide arc with slight upturn at corners; teeth visible (4–6) in Grade 1+ |
| Chin | Lifted 5–8px upward from neutral position |
| Chest | Character posture changes to match: chest forward, shoulders back |
| Cheek | Prominent rosy circles (2 circles per cheek) |

### 04 — Curious

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | One eye slightly narrowed (75% of full size); one fully open |
| Brow | Asymmetrical: one brow raised, one neutral or slightly lowered |
| Mouth | Small closed arc; slight downward pinch at one corner |
| Head tilt | 10–15° rotation of the entire head (applied as SVG transform) |
| Overall | The asymmetry is key — it reads as genuine curiosity |

### 05 — Concentrating

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Slightly narrowed (85% of default height); looking downward or at the task |
| Brow | Very slight inner-brow furrow (brow paths move 2px closer together at centre) |
| Mouth | Neutral horizontal line, very slight downward curve at centre; closed |
| Overall | Read as effort, not distress. Must never look unhappy |

### 06 — Surprised

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Maximum size (125% of default); pupils centred; wide open |
| Brow | Both brows raised to maximum height; arched outward |
| Mouth | Small open oval (not arc); shows small black interior |
| Overall | Arms may be raised or hands at cheeks to amplify the expression |

### 07 — Encouraging

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Happy default; one eye may have a slight wink (partially closed, arc-shaped) |
| Brow | Relaxed upward arch; warm |
| Mouth | Wide gentle smile; similar to Happy but with slightly more open quality |
| Arms | One arm extended, pointing or giving thumbs up |
| Overall | Body language drives this expression as much as the face |

### 08 — Thinking

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Directed upward-left (classic recall/thinking direction) |
| Brow | One side slightly raised; other side neutral |
| Mouth | Small closed curve, slightly asymmetric |
| Hand | One hand near chin or temple |
| Overall | Conveys active cognition; never looks confused or lost |

### 09 — Welcoming

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Eyes | Gentle happy; may have slight "soft" narrowing (warmth, not suspicion) |
| Brow | Relaxed, neutral arch |
| Mouth | Gentle, medium-width smile |
| Arms | Both arms slightly extended or one arm open-gestured |
| Overall | Reads as "come in, you are safe here" |

## 7.3 Forbidden Expressions

The following expressions are never used in any Worksheet Wonder product:

| Forbidden Expression | Reason |
|---------------------|--------|
| Crying | Creates negative emotional association with learning |
| Angry / aggressive | Frightening; inappropriate |
| Disgusted / revolted | Negative; culturally variable |
| Mocking or sneering | Directly contradicts our emotional contract |
| Fear / terror | Traumatising for anxious children |
| Blank / expressionless | Cold; lacks engagement |
| Smug or superior | Creates hierarchy and exclusion |

# CHAPTER 08 — EYE DESIGN SYSTEM

## 8.1 Eyes Are the Soul of the Character

In Worksheet Wonder illustrations, the eyes are the most expressive and most carefully specified element of any character. Poorly drawn eyes undermine the entire character. A child reads the eyes first — before body, before context, before anything else.

## 8.2 The Standard Eye Anatomy

The Worksheet Wonder eye consists of five components:

```
COMPONENT          ELEMENT                SIZE REFERENCE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Eye white       circle/ellipse         r = 1.0 unit (reference)
2. Iris            filled circle          r = 0.65 units
3. Pupil           filled dark circle     r = 0.35 units
4. Highlight       small filled circle    r = 0.12 units (top-right position)
5. Lash/lid line   stroke arc above eye   2–2.5px stroke weight
```

## 8.3 Eye Geometry Specifications

### Standard Child Eye (Happy/Default)

```
Eye white:
  shape:           circle or slight vertical ellipse
  fill:            #FFFFFF (white)
  stroke:          #1a1a1a
  stroke-width:    2px
  rx / ry:         equal (circle) or rx < ry by 5% (slight vertical stretch)

Iris:
  fill:            varies by character (see colour system)
  stroke:          none
  position:        centred within eye white
  size:            65% of eye white diameter

Pupil:
  fill:            #1a1a1a
  stroke:          none
  position:        centred within iris
  size:            35% of eye white diameter

Highlight:
  fill:            #FFFFFF
  stroke:          none
  position:        10–11 o'clock within pupil
  size:            12% of eye white diameter

Upper lid line:
  shape:           arc slightly flatter than eye white top curve
  stroke:          #1a1a1a
  stroke-width:    2–2.5px
  stroke-linecap:  round
  position:        2px above top of eye white
  extent:          slightly wider than eye white (adds definition)
```

### Eye Spacing Rule

```
Inter-eye distance (centre to centre):   1.6–1.8 × eye diameter
Eye position on face:                    centred horizontally at 45% from top of head
Eye width:                               each eye = 18–22% of total face width
```

### Eye Direction Standards

| Gaze Direction | Technical Implementation |
|----------------|--------------------------|
| Forward (neutral) | Pupil centred in iris |
| Looking right | Pupil shifted 15–20% rightward in iris |
| Looking left | Pupil shifted 15–20% leftward |
| Looking up | Pupil shifted 20% upward |
| Looking at task | Downward 15% + slight rightward |
| Wink | Upper lid arc path replaces entire eye; single downward stroke |

## 8.4 Eye Variations by Age Representation

| Character Age | Eye Size (relative to face) | Iris/Pupil Ratio | Notes |
|---------------|-----------------------------|-----------------|-------|
| Toddler (2–3) | 28–32% of face height | Large pupil (45%) | Maximally endearing |
| Young child (3–7) | 24–28% | Standard (35%) | Core style |
| Older child (7–12) | 20–24% | Standard (35%) | Slightly more realistic |
| Adult | 16–20% | Smaller pupil (28%) | More naturalistic |
| Animal | 30–40% | Very large pupil (50%) | Maximum cuteness |

## 8.5 Iris Colour Standards (Colour Version)

The iris colour does not affect the design in B&W printing (all irises appear dark grey). In colour versions:

| Iris Colour | Hex | Usage |
|-------------|-----|-------|
| Warm brown | #7B4A2D | Default human character |
| Deep brown | #3D1F0A | Melanin-rich characters |
| Hazel | #8B6914 | Alternative warm tone |
| Grey-blue | #6B8FA8 | Fair-skinned characters |
| Green | #5C8A5C | Character differentiation |
| Amber | #A07828 | Warm differentiation |

All irises always have a dark pupil (#1a1a1a) and white highlight regardless of iris colour.

## 8.6 Eyelash Standards

Eyelashes are used sparingly and only in the following contexts:
- Female-coded animal characters (where lashes distinguish gender)
- Stylised characters where lashes add personality (not realism)

**When used:**
- Maximum 3 lash strokes per eye
- Stroke weight: 1.5px
- Stroke length: equal to 20% of eye white radius
- Angle: radiating from upper-outer quadrant
- Never use "thick band of lashes" — this reads as adult/fashion

**Never use on:** Child human characters (gender-neutral by default)

# CHAPTER 09 — MOUTH DESIGN SYSTEM

## 9.1 The Mouth as Emotional Dial

If the eyes are the soul of the character, the mouth is the emotional dial. It moves more dramatically than any other feature and communicates the precise degree of happiness, concentration, or surprise with remarkable precision. A 5-pixel change in the curve of a mouth arc changes the entire reading of a character.

## 9.2 Mouth Geometry Reference System

All Worksheet Wonder mouths are defined within a **mouth bounding box** measured relative to the face:

```
Mouth bounding box width:    30–40% of face width (varies by expression)
Mouth bounding box height:   8–20% of face height (varies by expression)
Mouth position:              78% from top of head
```

## 9.3 The Mouth Library

### Type 1 — Simple Upward Arc (Happy / Default)

```
Path type:       Quadratic Bézier arc (open, no fill)
Control point:   Downward (creates upward curve)
Stroke:          #1a1a1a
Stroke-width:    2.5px (ages 3–5) / 2px (ages 5–12)
Linecap:         round
Width:           30% of face width
Curvature:       Control point Y offset = 30–40% of face width (moderate curve)
Fill:            none (open arc, no interior)
```

**Usage:** Happy, Welcoming, Encouraging expressions

### Type 2 — Wide Open Arc (Excited / Proud)

```
Path type:       Quadratic Bézier arc (may be closed at top = D-shape)
Width:           40–50% of face width
Curvature:       Control point Y offset = 40–50% (deeper curve = bigger smile)
Interior:        When closed: white teeth area fill (#FFFFFF)
Teeth:           2–6 rounded rectangles (rx=2); equal width; never more than 6
Tongue:          Not used in child characters; optional in animal characters
```

### Type 3 — Closed Neutral Line (Concentrating / Thinking)

```
Path type:       Straight line or very slight curve
Width:           20–25% of face width
Curvature:       0 (neutral) or control point offset = 5% (very slight)
Stroke:          #1a1a1a
Stroke-width:    2px
Fill:            none
```

**Usage:** Concentrating, Thinking — never used as a "default" (implies boredom)

### Type 4 — Small Open Oval (Surprised)

```
Shape:           Ellipse, slightly taller than wide
Width:           12–18% of face width
Height:          15–22% of face width
Fill:            #1a1a1a (dark interior — creates "open mouth" reading)
Stroke:          none (or very light)
Position:        Standard mouth position
```

### Type 5 — Wavy / Wobbly Arc (Uncertain / Thinking with emotion)

```
Path type:       Cubic Bézier with two control points creating S-curve
Width:           25–30% of face width
Usage:           The "Curious" expression only
Curvature:       First half curves up slightly; second half curves down slightly
```

### Type 6 — Teeth-Showing Wide Arc (Celebrating / Grade 1+)

```
Path type:       Quadratic Bézier arc; CLOSED (D-shape)
Width:           45–55% of face width
Upper lip:       1.5px stroke arc
Lower lip:       Straight baseline of D-shape
Teeth fill:      White rectangle with thin vertical dividers (1px)
Teeth count:     4–8; always even number
Used from:       Senior KG characters and above only
```

## 9.4 Mouth Forbidden Practices

| Forbidden | Reason |
|-----------|--------|
| Mouth with more than 8 visible teeth | Creates unsettling impression |
| Tongue prominently visible | Inappropriate for educational context |
| Asymmetric drooping mouth | Reads as illness, distress, or disability signal |
| Extremely thin mouth line (below 1px) | Disappears in print |
| Mouth touching the chin or edge of face | Proportion violation |
| Triangular open mouth shape | Reads as aggressive or animal-predator |

# CHAPTER 10 — HAND & FOOT STYLE

## 10.1 Hands Are Hard — So We Have a System

Hands are notoriously difficult to illustrate. In educational illustration, there are two additional problems: hands must be simple enough to reproduce at small print sizes, and they must be legible in black-and-white. Worksheet Wonder resolves this with a strictly codified hand vocabulary.

## 10.2 The Four Hand Types

Worksheet Wonder uses exactly four hand types. No other hand forms are used without Art Director approval.

### Hand Type A — The Mitten Hand (Preschool–Junior KG content)

```
Description:     A rounded, simple mitten shape with no individual fingers
Shape:           An organic blob — circular base with a thumb bump
Thumb:           Single rounded protrusion at upper-left of the mitten
Wrist:           Short cylindrical sleeve above
Stroke:          2.5px (matches character outline weight)
Fill:            Same as character skin tone (or white in B&W)
Use context:     Toddler and Preschool characters
Detail:          None — no finger lines, no knuckles, no nails
```

### Hand Type B — The Simplified Three-Finger Hand (Senior KG–Grade 2 content)

```
Description:     A hand with thumb + three merged fingers (not four separate)
Fingers:         Three rounded bumps along the top; NOT separately defined
Thumb:           Distinct, slightly separated from fingers
Detail:          Single crease line on palm (1px dashed or light stroke)
Stroke:          2px
Fill:            Skin tone or white in B&W
Use context:     Mid-age character range; when hand action is relevant
```

### Hand Type C — The Four-Finger Hand (Grade 3–Grade 8 content)

```
Description:     Standard cartoon hand with four fingers + thumb
Fingers:         Four distinct rounded fingers with slight tapering
Thumb:           Fully separate; appropriate length
Knuckle lines:   Optional; 1px curved lines at first knuckle if included
Nail detail:     NOT included in standard illustrations; only in science anatomy
Stroke:          2px
Fill:            Skin tone or white in B&W
Use context:     Older student characters; demonstration/instructional hands
```

### Hand Type D — The Action Hand (All ages, specific poses)

```
Description:     A simplified hand frozen in a specific recognisable action
Actions:         Thumbs up / Pointing finger / Wave / Open palm / Pencil grip
Construction:    Built specifically for each action; not a general-purpose hand
Detail:          Action-specific (e.g., pointing index finger fully extended)
Use context:     Reward stickers, instruction arrows, encouragement elements
```

## 10.3 Hand Proportion Standards

```
Hand height (excluding wrist): 25% of character's total head diameter
Hand width:                    20% of character's total head diameter
Wrist diameter:                50% of hand width
Finger length (Type C):        55% of palm height
Thumb length:                  35% of palm height, placed at lower 60% of hand
```

## 10.4 Foot Style Standards

### Standard Foot (All Ages)

```
Shape:           Elongated oval; pointed-oval or teardrop for shoe silhouette
Length:          30% of character's lower leg length
Width:           45% of foot length
Heel:            Rounded protrusion at the rear
Toe area:        Blunt round front
Shoe presence:   Characters always wear shoes (not bare feet) except in:
                 - Bath/beach scenes
                 - Specific cultural contexts (noted in brief)
Shoe colour:     Varies; always contrasted with ground surface
Sole line:       1px stroke along the bottom of the shoe (distinguishes sole)
Laces:           Optional; 2 small cross-hatch strokes for Grade 1+ characters
```

### Simplified Foot (Preschool characters)

```
Shape:           A simple rounded block — no heel detail, no shoe structure
Color:           Flat fill contrasting with trouser/leg
Length:          25% of lower leg
Detail:          None
```

# CHAPTER 11 — LINE WEIGHT STANDARDS

## 11.1 Why Line Weight Is a Design Decision

Line weight is one of the most powerful tools in illustration. It establishes hierarchy — it tells the eye which elements are primary, which are secondary, and which are background. In educational illustration, this hierarchy is especially important because the child's eye must be guided precisely to the learning-relevant elements of each illustration.

## 11.2 The Five-Level Line Weight System

Worksheet Wonder uses exactly five line weight levels. Every stroke in every illustration is assigned to one of these levels.

| Level | Name | Weight | Colour | Used For |
|-------|------|--------|--------|---------|
| L1 | **Primary Outline** | 2.5px | #1a1a1a | Character outer silhouette; primary object outline |
| L2 | **Secondary Outline** | 2.0px | #1a1a1a | Character inner divisions (head/body), major feature outlines |
| L3 | **Interior Detail** | 1.5px | #333333 | Eyes, mouth detail, clothing lines, major surface detail |
| L4 | **Subtle Detail** | 1.0px | #555555 | Minor texture, pattern suggestion, secondary surface detail |
| L5 | **Ghost / Guide** | 0.75px | #888888 | Background elements, watermark, structural guides |

## 11.3 Line Weight by Age Range

The absolute line weights above are calibrated for the core Worksheet Wonder style. For age-specific content, scale as follows:

| Age Range | L1 Weight | L2 Weight | L3 Weight | L4 Weight | L5 Weight |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Preschool (3–4) | 3.0px | 2.5px | 2.0px | 1.5px | 1.0px |
| Junior KG (4–5) | 2.75px | 2.25px | 1.75px | 1.25px | 0.75px |
| Senior KG (5–6) | 2.5px | 2.0px | 1.5px | 1.0px | 0.75px |
| Grade 1–2 | 2.5px | 2.0px | 1.5px | 1.0px | 0.75px |
| Grade 3–5 | 2.25px | 1.75px | 1.25px | 1.0px | 0.5px |
| Grade 6–8 | 2.0px | 1.5px | 1.0px | 0.75px | 0.5px |

## 11.4 Line Weight Application Rules

### Rule 1 — No Level Skipping in the Same Element
Within a single illustration, line weights must follow the hierarchy. You cannot have an L3 line that is heavier than an L1 line on the same character.

### Rule 2 — Consistency Within the Same Element Type
All character outlines in a single scene use the same L1 weight. All clothing lines use the same L3 weight. There is no "thick outline on the hero, thin outline on the background character."

### Rule 3 — Line Weight and Distance
Background elements use L5 weights regardless of their conceptual importance. This creates the illusion of atmospheric depth without requiring complex rendering.

### Rule 4 — Tracing Lines Are Separate from Illustration Lines
Tracing guide lines (the dashed lines children trace) are governed by the Tracing Line Standards in the Design Bible (Chapter 13), not by this illustration line weight system. They coexist on the same page but operate independently.

### Rule 5 — No Tapered Lines
Worksheet Wonder does not use calligraphic tapered strokes (strokes that change width along their length). All strokes are uniform weight. This is enforced through the use of `stroke-width` as a fixed value, never as a variable profile.

## 11.5 SVG Stroke Attributes — Mandatory Global Settings

All strokes in every Worksheet Wonder SVG must include:

```xml
stroke-linecap="round"
stroke-linejoin="round"
```

These two attributes are responsible for approximately 40% of the "warm, friendly" quality of the Worksheet Wonder line. Without them, strokes are cold and mechanical. With them, they breathe.

# CHAPTER 12 — SVG CODING STANDARDS

## 12.1 Why SVG Is the Mandated Format

All Worksheet Wonder illustrations are created and distributed in SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics) format. This is non-negotiable. SVG is mandated because:

1. **Resolution independence** — Prints perfectly at 300 DPI A4 and scales to billboard without degradation
2. **Accessibility** — Supports `aria-label`, `title`, and `desc` elements for screen readers
3. **Editability** — Can be styled, modified, and customised without image editing software
4. **Portability** — Embeds directly in HTML worksheets without external dependencies
5. **File size efficiency** — Complex illustrations at a fraction of raster file sizes

## 12.2 Required SVG Header Structure

Every Worksheet Wonder SVG file must open with the following structure:

```xml
<svg
  xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg"
  xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink"
  viewBox="0 0 [width] [height]"
  width="[width]"
  height="[height]"
  role="img"
  aria-labelledby="title-[id] desc-[id]"
>
  <title id="title-[id]">[Brief title of illustration]</title>
  <desc id="desc-[id]">[Full accessibility description]</desc>
  <!-- illustration content -->
</svg>
```

**Notes:**
- `viewBox` always starts at `0 0`
- `width` and `height` are set to the intended render dimensions (overridden by CSS when embedded)
- `role="img"` is mandatory for accessibility
- Both `<title>` and `<desc>` are mandatory for every illustration SVG

## 12.3 Viewbox Coordinate System

All Worksheet Wonder illustrations are built on a **standardised coordinate grid**:

| Illustration Type | ViewBox Width | ViewBox Height | Use |
|------------------|---------------|----------------|-----|
| Icon (small) | 64 | 64 | Badges, small activity icons |
| Standard | 120 | 120 | Row illustrations, inline characters |
| Medium | 200 | 200 | Section illustrations, title block art |
| Large | 300 | 300 | Cover illustrations, full-section art |
| Wide banner | 400 | 150 | Header scenes, landscape panoramas |
| Full page | 595 | 842 | Full A4 illustrations |

**Rule:** Never build an illustration at an arbitrary viewBox size. Always use one of the above six standard grids. This ensures consistent scaling across the catalogue.

## 12.4 Path Construction Standards

### Use Absolute Coordinates
All path data uses **absolute coordinates** (uppercase commands), never relative coordinates. This makes paths readable, debuggable, and maintainable.

```xml
<!-- CORRECT — absolute coordinates -->
<path d="M 30 50 C 30 30, 70 30, 70 50" />

<!-- FORBIDDEN — relative coordinates for complex paths -->
<path d="m 30 50 c 0 -20, 40 -20, 40 0" />
```

### Curve Standards
- **Bézier curves:** Use `C` (cubic) for character outlines; `Q` (quadratic) for simple arcs
- **Arcs:** Use `A` for circular arcs within shapes; specify exact radii
- **Straight lines:** Use `L`; avoid `H` and `V` for paths that may need editing

### Shape Primitives First
Where a path can be replaced by an SVG primitive, use the primitive:

```xml
<!-- PREFERRED — SVG primitive -->
<circle cx="60" cy="40" r="20" />
<rect x="10" y="10" width="80" height="60" rx="8" />
<ellipse cx="60" cy="80" rx="30" ry="15" />

<!-- AVOID unless shape is complex -->
<path d="M 40 40 A 20 20 0 1 1 80 40 A 20 20 0 1 1 40 40 Z" />
```

## 12.5 Group Structure Standards

All illustrations must be organised into logical SVG groups using `<g>` elements with descriptive `id` attributes:

```xml
<svg viewBox="0 0 120 120" ...>
  <g id="background">
    <!-- background elements -->
  </g>
  <g id="character-body">
    <g id="legs">...</g>
    <g id="torso">...</g>
    <g id="arms">...</g>
    <g id="head">
      <g id="face">
        <g id="eyes">...</g>
        <g id="mouth">...</g>
        <g id="nose">...</g>
      </g>
    </g>
  </g>
  <g id="props">
    <!-- objects held or nearby -->
  </g>
  <g id="foreground">
    <!-- elements in front of character -->
  </g>
</svg>
```

## 12.6 Forbidden SVG Practices

| Practice | Reason | Correct Alternative |
|----------|--------|-------------------|
| Inline style attributes | Not maintainable; breaks CSS theming | Use class attributes |
| `style="..."` on individual elements | Same as above | Use class or presentation attributes |
| `<image>` elements embedding raster | Defeats SVG purpose | Recreate in SVG |
| Deeply nested transforms | Creates rendering bugs | Flatten to absolute coordinates |
| Zero-width/height groups | Invisible; causes confusion | Remove unused groups |
| Path data without spaces between coordinates | Unreadable | Always space-separate |
| Missing `role="img"` | Accessibility failure | Always include |
| Missing `<title>` or `<desc>` | Accessibility failure | Always include |
| `filter` elements (drop shadows, blurs) | Print rendering inconsistency | Never use |
| `opacity` below 0.15 on any visible element | Disappears in print | Use colour equivalent |

## 12.7 CSS Class System for SVG

All Worksheet Wonder SVG elements use a standardised CSS class naming system:

```css
/* Stroke classes */
.ww-stroke-l1  { stroke: #1a1a1a; stroke-width: 2.5px; }
.ww-stroke-l2  { stroke: #1a1a1a; stroke-width: 2.0px; }
.ww-stroke-l3  { stroke: #333;    stroke-width: 1.5px; }
.ww-stroke-l4  { stroke: #555;    stroke-width: 1.0px; }
.ww-stroke-l5  { stroke: #888;    stroke-width: 0.75px; }

/* Fill classes — B&W mode */
.ww-fill-white  { fill: #FFFFFF; }
.ww-fill-light  { fill: #F0F0F0; }
.ww-fill-mid    { fill: #CCCCCC; }
.ww-fill-dark   { fill: #555555; }
.ww-fill-black  { fill: #1a1a1a; }
.ww-fill-none   { fill: none; }

/* Universal round caps — applied to all strokes */
.ww-round       { stroke-linecap: round; stroke-linejoin: round; }

/* Character component classes */
.ww-char-outline  { } /* extends ww-stroke-l1 + ww-round */
.ww-char-detail   { } /* extends ww-stroke-l3 + ww-round */
.ww-eye-white     { fill: #FFFFFF; stroke: #1a1a1a; stroke-width: 2px; }
.ww-eye-iris      { fill: #555; stroke: none; }
.ww-eye-pupil     { fill: #1a1a1a; stroke: none; }
.ww-eye-highlight { fill: #FFFFFF; stroke: none; }
.ww-mouth         { fill: none; stroke: #1a1a1a; stroke-linecap: round; }
```

# CHAPTER 13 — LAYER NAMING STANDARDS

## 13.1 Why Layer Naming Matters

Well-named layers are the difference between an illustration that one person can create and one that a whole studio can maintain, update, and hand off without confusion. Every Worksheet Wonder SVG is named so that any team member can open it and immediately understand its structure.

## 13.2 The Layer Naming Convention

```
[category]--[subject]--[descriptor]

Examples:
  bg--sky--gradient-top
  char--wonder--body
  char--wonder--head
  char--wonder--face--eyes
  char--wonder--face--mouth
  prop--pencil--main
  fx--shadow--ground
  text--label--top
```

## 13.3 Category Prefix Reference

| Prefix | Category |
|--------|----------|
| `bg` | Background elements |
| `char` | Character (human or animal) |
| `prop` | Props, objects, tools |
| `env` | Environment elements (ground, sky, water) |
| `fx` | Visual effects (shadows, highlights, particles) |
| `text` | Text elements within the illustration |
| `ui` | User interface elements (borders, frames, badges) |
| `guide` | Construction guides (not visible in final output) |
| `mask` | Clipping mask elements |

## 13.4 Layer Order (Bottom to Top)

The rendering order in every SVG must follow this sequence:

```
Layer 01 — bg (backgrounds, sky, ground wash)
Layer 02 — env (environment details: hills, water, ground texture)
Layer 03 — bg-char (characters in the background / far plane)
Layer 04 — prop-bg (props in background plane)
Layer 05 — char-shadow (ground shadows of main characters)
Layer 06 — char-body (main character bodies)
Layer 07 — char-detail (main character details, clothing, accessories)
Layer 08 — char-face (faces: eyes, mouth, expression)
Layer 09 — prop-fg (props in foreground / held by character)
Layer 10 — env-fg (foreground environmental elements)
Layer 11 — fx (effects: sparkles, motion lines, highlights)
Layer 12 — ui (frames, borders, labels, badges)
Layer 13 — text (any text within the illustration frame)
Layer 14 — guide (construction guides — HIDDEN in final output)
```

## 13.5 Sublayer Naming Within Characters

When a character is complex enough to require sublayers:

```
char--[name]--shadow
char--[name]--body
char--[name]--clothing
char--[name]--limbs--left-arm
char--[name]--limbs--right-arm
char--[name]--limbs--left-leg
char--[name]--limbs--right-leg
char--[name]--head--shape
char--[name]--head--hair
char--[name]--face--brow
char--[name]--face--eyes--left
char--[name]--face--eyes--right
char--[name]--face--nose
char--[name]--face--mouth
char--[name]--face--cheeks
char--[name]--accessories
```

# CHAPTER 15 — BLACK & WHITE PRINTING STANDARDS

## 15.1 B&W Is Primary — Always

Every illustration created for Worksheet Wonder must be designed first and foremost for black-and-white printing. Colour is a secondary enhancement. If an illustration only works in colour, it has not been properly designed.

The reason is practical: the overwhelming majority of Worksheet Wonder customers print on home laser or inkjet printers on standard white paper. If the illustration does not read clearly in B&W, it fails the product.

## 15.2 The B&W Tonal System

In B&W output, colour is replaced by tone. Worksheet Wonder uses a five-tone B&W system:

| Tone Name | Hex Value | % Black | Visual Role |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Black | #1a1a1a | ~90% | Primary outlines, text |
| Dark | #555555 | ~67% | Secondary outlines, interior detail |
| Mid | #888888 | ~47% | Tertiary detail, background outlines |
| Light | #cccccc | ~20% | Light fills, shadow suggestions |
| Near-white | #f0f0f0 | ~6% | Interior fills, sky, ground plane |
| White | #ffffff | 0% | Writing spaces, highlights, page |

**Rule:** No two adjacent areas may be the same tone unless separated by a stroke of a different tone. Adjacent areas with no tonal distinction will merge visually on print.

## 15.3 Fill Rules for B&W

| Illustration Element | B&W Fill |
|---------------------|---------|
| Character skin | #f0f0f0 (near-white) — suggests skin without overpowering |
| Character hair | #888888 (mid-grey) — dark enough to contrast with face |
| Character clothing (primary) | #cccccc (light) |
| Character clothing (secondary/accent) | #f0f0f0 or white with stripe/pattern |
| Character shoes | #555555 (dark) |
| Background sky | White or #f8f8f8 |
| Ground / grass | #f0f0f0 with simple texture stroke |
| Water | Horizontal stroke pattern on white |
| Wood / earth | Diagonal hatch pattern on #f0f0f0 |
| Metal / hard objects | #cccccc with specular highlights (white patches) |

## 15.4 The Print Contrast Test

Before any illustration is approved for print:

**Step 1:** Convert the SVG to grayscale rendering (remove all colour fills, apply B&W equivalents)
**Step 2:** Print on standard 80gsm white paper using a mid-range inkjet printer at default settings
**Step 3:** Hold the printed page at arm's length
**Step 4:** Every element must be clearly readable at this distance

If any element is unclear, too faint, or merges with adjacent elements, the illustration must be revised.

## 15.5 Anti-Patterns That Fail in B&W Print

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Fix |
|-------------|---------|-----|
| Two different hues at similar lightness | Merge on B&W output | Increase tonal difference to ≥ 30% |
| Very light fill (above #f5f5f5) on white background | Invisible | Add a stroke border |
| Small text on grey fill | Loses contrast | Use #1a1a1a text on #ffffff or very light fill |
| Fine detail (below 0.75px) | Disappears or bleeds | Minimum 1px for visible detail |
| Adjacent characters with same skin tone fill | No distinction | Add darker stroke or hatching to one |
| Coloured pattern on coloured background | Both turn same grey | Use tonal contrast deliberately |

## 15.6 Ink Economy

Worksheet Wonder worksheets are printed by individual families and teachers using their own ink and toner. Therefore:

- **Never fill large background areas with heavy grey** (fill above #888888 on areas larger than 30% of page)
- **Avoid dark fills on decorative elements** that serve no functional purpose
- **Maximum ink coverage guideline:** No more than 35% of the total illustration area should be filled with tones darker than #cccccc

### Contrast Requirements
All text on coloured backgrounds must achieve WCAG AA contrast ratio (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text).

# CHAPTER 17 — ACCESSIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS

## 17.1 Accessibility Is Not Optional

Accessibility in illustration design is not a compliance exercise. It is the ethical baseline for a brand that serves children of all abilities. Some of the children using our worksheets have low vision, colour vision deficiency, or cognitive processing differences. Our illustrations must serve them.

## 17.2 Colour Vision Deficiency

Approximately 8% of males and 0.5% of females experience colour vision deficiency (CVD). The most common form is red-green deficiency (deuteranopia/protanopia).

**Mandatory test:** Every colour illustration must be tested using a CVD simulation tool before approval.

**Design rules to prevent CVD failures:**
- Never use colour alone to convey instructional information
- When two items must be distinguished by colour, they must also differ by pattern, shape, or position
- Avoid red-green adjacency as the sole differentiating factor
- Blue and yellow (our primary palette) are highly accessible to all CVD types

**CVD-Safe colour pairs:**

| Pair | Accessible to |
|------|--------------|
| Blue + Yellow | All CVD types |
| Blue + Orange | All CVD types |
| Dark Blue + Light Grey | All CVD types |
| Black + White | All CVD types |
| Purple + Green | Most CVD types (not all) |
| Red + Green | NOT accessible — avoid as sole differentiator |

## 17.3 Low Vision Accessibility

For children with low vision who may be using worksheets at enlarged scale:

- All character features must be legible at 200% scale
- No essential detail should require print size smaller than 4mm × 4mm
- All illustrations must retain their meaning at high zoom levels
- Do not use very light fills (#f5f5f5 or lighter) as the primary fill of any significant element

## 17.4 Cognitive Accessibility

For children with cognitive processing differences (ADHD, autism spectrum, dyslexia):

**Clarity over complexity:** When cognitive load is already high (difficult academic task), the accompanying illustration must be especially simple. The illustration should reduce cognitive load, not add to it.

**No hidden meanings:** All visual communications are literal. No sarcasm, no irony, no "trick" visual jokes that require sophisticated interpretation.

**Predictable iconography:** Once an icon means something (🎯 = learning objective), it never means anything else. Consistency is a cognitive support.

**Motion and animation:** In digital SVG applications, animated elements must include `prefers-reduced-motion` CSS media query support. Never auto-animate an element that is adjacent to a reading or writing task.

## 17.5 SVG Accessibility Code Requirements

```xml
<!-- REQUIRED on every illustration SVG -->
<svg role="img" aria-labelledby="svg-title svg-desc" ...>

  <!-- REQUIRED: brief, meaningful title -->
  <title id="svg-title">Child tracing a horizontal line toward a train station</title>

  <!-- REQUIRED: full description of educational context -->
  <desc id="svg-desc">
    An illustration showing a smiling child character seated at a desk,
    holding a pencil. A dashed horizontal line leads from the pencil tip
    to a small train station building on the right side of the image.
    The activity asks the child to trace the line from left to right.
  </desc>

  <!-- Purely decorative elements get aria-hidden -->
  <g aria-hidden="true" id="decorative-stars">
    <!-- star decorations -->
  </g>

</svg>
```

## 17.6 Print Accessibility

For children using printed worksheets:

| Requirement | Standard |
|-------------|----------|
| Minimum recognisable element size | 8mm × 8mm at intended print size |
| Minimum character face size | 15mm height |
| Minimum functional icon size | 10mm × 10mm |
| Outline weight minimum | 1.5px at 300 DPI output |
| Clear figure-ground separation | All characters/objects clearly separate from backgrounds |

# CHAPTER 18 — QUALITY CHECKLIST

## 18.1 Purpose

This checklist is completed by the illustrator and reviewed by the Art Director before any illustration is approved for use in a product. An illustration fails if any item is marked FAIL.

---

## 18.2 PHILOSOPHY & INTENT CHECKLIST

```
ILLUSTRATION PURPOSE
[ ] The illustration serves at least one of the four purpose hierarchy levels
    (Functional / Contextual / Navigational / Decorative)
[ ] The primary purpose is clearly identifiable
[ ] The illustration does not compete with the activity it accompanies
[ ] The illustration contributes to, or at minimum does not undermine,
    the stated learning objective

EMOTIONAL CONTRACT
[ ] The illustration is safe — nothing frightening, threatening, or disturbing
[ ] The illustration is warm — rounded forms, friendly proportions
[ ] The illustration is clear — primary subject identifiable in under 1 second
[ ] The illustration is joyful — character is active, engaged, or expressive
```

---

## 18.3 DESIGN STANDARDS CHECKLIST

```
SHAPE LANGUAGE
[ ] All shapes follow the shape restriction rules (no acute sharp angles
    on friendly/character elements)
[ ] All geometric shapes have appropriate softening (rx/ry on rect elements)
[ ] Shape language is appropriate for the subject matter

CHARACTER PROPORTIONS
[ ] Character head-to-body ratio follows Chapter 06 standards
[ ] Eye size is appropriate for the character's represented age
[ ] Character posture communicates the correct emotional state

FACIAL EXPRESSIONS
[ ] Expression is one of the nine approved expressions (Chapter 07)
[ ] Expression matches the page's emotional tone
[ ] No forbidden expression is used

EYE DESIGN
[ ] Five-component eye structure present (white, iris, pupil, highlight, lid line)
[ ] Eye spacing follows the 1.6–1.8x diameter rule
[ ] Eye direction matches character's narrative action
[ ] Eye size is appropriate for character age

MOUTH DESIGN
[ ] Mouth type is from the approved library (Chapter 09 Types 1–6)
[ ] Mouth size falls within the bounding box specification
[ ] Mouth stroke uses stroke-linecap="round"
[ ] No forbidden mouth configuration is used

HAND & FOOT DESIGN
[ ] Hand type is appropriate for the character's represented age
[ ] Hand proportions follow Chapter 10 standards
[ ] Feet are present and appropriately styled (no bare feet unless contextual)

LINE WEIGHT
[ ] All strokes are assigned to one of the five line weight levels (L1–L5)
[ ] L1 is the heaviest stroke; no detail line is heavier than the primary outline
[ ] All strokes use stroke-linecap="round" and stroke-linejoin="round"
[ ] No tapered/calligraphic strokes
```

---

## 18.4 TECHNICAL STANDARDS CHECKLIST

```
SVG STRUCTURE
[ ] SVG opens with correct required header (viewBox, role, aria-labelledby)
[ ] <title> element present with meaningful text
[ ] <desc> element present with full accessibility description
[ ] viewBox uses one of the six standard coordinate grids (Chapter 12.3)
[ ] All paths use absolute coordinates (uppercase path commands)

GROUP STRUCTURE
[ ] All elements are organised in named <g> groups
[ ] Group ids follow the layer naming convention (Chapter 13)
[ ] Rendering layer order follows the standard (bg → env → char → props → fx → ui)
[ ] No empty or unnecessary groups remain

CODE QUALITY
[ ] No relative coordinates in complex paths
[ ] No <image> elements embedding raster content
[ ] No filter elements (drop shadow, blur, etc.)
[ ] No opacity below 0.15 on any visible element
[ ] All primitives (circles, rects) used where paths could be avoided
[ ] CSS classes used for all reusable style attributes

FILE NAMING
[ ] Filename follows WW--[CATEGORY]--[SUBJECT]--[DESCRIPTOR]--[VARIANT].svg
[ ] All segments are lowercase with hyphens
[ ] Category code is from the approved list
```

```
[ ] All adjacent areas have sufficient tonal contrast (≥ 30% lightness difference)

[ ] No fill used that is darker than #555 on background/decorative elements

[ ] No large solid black or dark-grey backgrounds
[ ] Illustration has been tested in grayscale rendering

PRINT LEGIBILITY

[ ] All elements legible at arm's length

[ ] All significant elements minimum 8mm × 8mm at print size
```

## Appendix A — Quick Reference: The Non-Negotiable Rules

```
NEVER:
  ✗ Sharp-angle shapes on character/friendly elements
  ✗ Frightening, aggressive, or negative expressions
  ✗ Tapered/calligraphic stroke widths
  ✗ stroke-linecap="butt" or stroke-linejoin="miter"
  ✗ Raster images embedded within SVG
  ✗ SVG filter effects (blur, shadow, glow)
  ✗ Pure RGB primaries (#F00, #0F0, #00F)
  ✗ Relative coordinates in complex path data
  ✗ Missing <title> or <desc> in any SVG
  ✗ Red/green as sole differentiator (CVD failure)
  ✗ Opacity below 0.15 on any visible element
  ✗ Comic book "violence" effects (cracks, explosions, impact stars)
  ✗ Any content that violates the child safety emotional contract

ALWAYS:
  ✓ stroke-linecap="round" + stroke-linejoin="round" on all strokes
  ✓ Five-level line weight hierarchy (L1 heaviest, L5 lightest)
  ✓ Role="img" + aria-labelledby on every SVG
  ✓ Rounded shapes for character and friendly elements
  ✓ One of six standard viewBox coordinate grids
  ✓ Absolute coordinates in path data
  ✓ Named <g> groups following layer convention
  ✓ Print test on 80gsm paper before approval
  ✓ CVD simulation test for colour versions
  ✓ Minimum 4 skin tones in any set of 6+ human characters
```
